## 1. The Universal Five-Beat Engine

Every film, every episode, every reel — whatever its length, market, or fantasy level — runs these five beats in this order. Beats may compress or share screen time; none may be skipped.

### B1 · THE QUESTION
Open inside a question the viewer already carries. Not a question we invent — one they walked in with. *Can one product really replace everything? How is your home always clean? What if guests arrive in 20 minutes? Who teaches care?*
The hook is never the product. The hook is the viewer's own life, met precisely.

### B2 · THE ORDINARY
The real world, documentary texture: concrete laundry basin, net curtain, plastic chairs, courtyard washing lines, township pitch at golden hour. The moment that matters approaches — hosting day, guests in 20 minutes, the graze on the knee, the morning the house isn't awake yet.
Nothing staged-glossy. If the audience lives it, we don't explain it.

### B3 · THE TURN (the hinge)
The product moment. The single point where the state changes — hesitation to action, panic to method, hurt to restored, many to one. The product is the physical act that marks the transition.
**The Removal Test governs this beat: cut the product scene — if the story still works, the product was decoration and the script fails.** The turn is the only beat the brand may own.

### B4 · THE PROOF
The changed world, shown. One claim per film, earned on screen, never spoken as a lecture: five surfaces with one bucket; the shield-grid house; the player scoring; the cupboard with one tub. The camera does the claiming.
Evidence before claim — always. If the proof needs a voiceover to land, the proof isn't there yet.

### B5 · THE RETURN
Back to reality, carrying the weight. The emotional payload lands — the nod that says *you know how to do this now*, the smile in the doorway, the child's arms up at the fence. Then one named emotion, one ask. Never two asks.

## 2. The Witness Principle

Every strong film in the estate has a **witness inside the story**: Ana in the doorway, the child at the fence, the child at door-handle height, the neighbours over the wall. The claim lands through the witness's eyes — so the viewer is never lectured, they *overhear* the truth.

Rule: if no character inside the story sees the change, the audience is being told, not shown. Add the witness or rewrite the beat.

Corollary — **the witness converts**: across a series, this episode's witness is next episode's practitioner (Glória watched, then owned it; the child watched, then inherited it). That handover IS the series arc.

---

## 3. The Fantasy Dial

Brands differ by one setting: how far B3/B4 may depart from literal reality.

| Setting | What it looks like | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| **0% — Documentary** | The proof is literally real: one bucket, five surfaces, a stopwatch | ACO |
| **50% — Heightened** | Reality, but the moment is composed as myth: golden light, the armouring read on a face | Ibucap (Kamau) |
| **100% — Believed magic** | The child's eyes take over: glow, shields, germ-creatures. The fantasy is sincere, never comic, and always RETURNS to reality in B5 | Germol |

Laws at every setting: the fantasy is the *witness's perception*, never the brand's claim; adults never see the magic; the return to reality is mandatory; no fear language, no germ close-ups, no horror register — the magic protects, it doesn't threaten.

---

## 4. Escalation by Subtraction

A series does not escalate by adding spectacle. It escalates by **removing props**: ACO removed the coaching, then the stopwatch, then the list, until only a reaching hand remained — and the quietest film carried the biggest claim (habit).

Rule: each episode should need *less* apparatus than the last to make its point. If episode N+1 needs more noise than episode N, the series is weakening, not building.

## 5. The Tests (every script passes all five)

1. **Removal Test** — cut the product beat; the story must break.
2. **Silence Test** — play B3–B5 with no dialogue; the beats must still read. Dialogue may decorate a beat, never carry it.
3. **Witness Test** — someone inside the story sees the change.
4. **Second-Watch Test** — one reward visible only on a rewatch (the theories all disproven, the rooms counted, the same shelf position).
5. **One-Claim Test** — name the film's single product truth in one sentence. If it takes two sentences, it's two films.

## 6. Universal Laws (carry into every brand derivation)

1. **Evidence before claim** — the estate spine, unchanged.
2. **One claim per film. One emotion named. One ask.**
3. **Documentary ground** — real homes, real streets, real hands; never explain the audience's own life to them.
4. **The product is the hinge, not the decoration** (Flodent's law, now universal).
5. **The witness carries the claim** (§2).
6. **Warmth is never mockery** — speculation, panic, and mistakes inside stories are affectionate; the audience must recognise themselves and feel seen, not judged.
7. **Local truth** — market textures (language, market stalls, laundry lines, football) are lived reality, not colour. Portuguese/Angola, SA registers, Kenya rules each carry their own gates.
8. **Compliance gates attach at derivation** — each brand bolts its regulatory laws (PPB/FDA bans, pack laws, claim locks, PT passes) onto the engine; the engine never overrides a gate.
9. **Sound is diegetic first** — the beats must work in the doc register; score is post, per production laws.
10. **Numbers and endorsements only when real** — no invented proof inside fiction: the demo must be actually performable.
