# Shaltoux — "Pass on the Warmth" — Competition Sprint Brief
**Brand:** Shaltoux (cough & throat lozenges) · **Format:** UGC competition · **Timeline:** 2-week build-up + 4-week competition
**Written:** 2026-07-13 · Niamh, seat 02

---

## 1 · The Story

*(Updated 2026-07-13 with Shane's note: contemporary, not traditional dress — plain everyday clothes, nothing costumed. Setting sits at the edge of an informal beachside settlement, textured and real, not a resort beach.)*

Two Nigerian teens, late teens, sitting on a low, weather-worn wall where the edge of a beachside settlement meets the shore — corrugated fencing, sun-faded paint, laundry lines, real texture and colour in the background. They want to look at each other — sly glances, nobody speaks first.

He takes out a Shaltoux. Green. It glows — comfort, offered quietly.

He checks what's left in the pack: one more, orange. His good one. Instead of eating it himself, he sets it on the wall and slides it across to her. Gets up. Walks off.

She picks it up, eats it. Glows orange — the scene's whole colour grade shifts warm, orange bleeding into the light.

Outro: the two of them walking down the beach together.

**Line:** *Pass on the Warmth.*
**Sub-line:** Shaltoux.
**Assets needed from entrants:** voice/dialogue only (or none — glances can carry it), end-card packshot + logo. That's the whole production requirement — deliberately cheap and fast to shoot on a phone, which is what makes it enterable at scale.

## 2 · What It Means to Consumers

The gift isn't the lozenge, it's the choice. He had two flavours left and gave her the better one. Comfort (green) is what he keeps for himself; energy (orange) is what he hands over. That's the whole emotional transaction — warmth passed from one person to another, dramatized through colour rather than dialogue.

This reframes Shaltoux from "thing you take when your throat hurts" to "thing you give someone you like." It's the first time the brand's mood-colour system (comfort=green, energy=orange, strong=yellow) is *shown* as a literal transformation rather than stated as a spec — the audience learns the code by watching it happen to a face, not by reading it off a pack.

## 3 · What It Does for the Brand

- **Youth relevance without going explicit.** Romantic tension carried entirely by glances and a small gesture — fits "Sound Like Yourself": the characters barely need lines.
- **Teaches the mood-colour system for free**, embedded in a love story instead of a product explainer.
- **UGC = cheap reach.** Every entrant reshoots the same two-beat structure with their own cast, location, and chemistry — the story travels through remix, not media spend. Low production bar means high entry volume.
- **Owns a single, ownable mechanic** (glow-on-eat, colour-graded scene shift) that's simple enough to become a recognisable Shaltoux signature if it lands.

## 4 · Mechanism Flow

**Build-up — 2 weeks before launch**
Seed the reference film (our own "Pass on the Warmth" cut) across channels, brief micro-influencers, tease the prize pool. No entries yet — this is pure anticipation.

**Competition — 4 weeks, each running a weekly cycle:**

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Monday | Post rules + call to action (re-anchors new entrants each week) |
| Wednesday | Release micro-influencer / collab content (fuels that week's entries) |
| Friday | Announce best 5 from the *previous* week's Sunday cutoff + award that week's prize; flag the upcoming Sunday as this week's cutoff |
| Sunday | Weekly submission window closes |

Entries submit continuously through the week; each Sunday's cutoff is judged and the top 5 announced the following Friday (a 5-day judging window). Repeats across all 4 competition weeks.

**Prizes:** $1,000 per week × 4 weeks, 5 winners per week. Split — **decided 2026-07-13, reversible on your word:** 1st $400 / 2nd $200 / 3rd $150 / 4th $150 / 5th $100. First place clearly ahead without making 2nd–5th feel like an afterthought.

**Voting — decided 2026-07-13:** internal judging picks the 5 winners each week, full stop — no hosted voting page, no separate build. Layer on a lightweight, no-cost "Audience Favourite" call-out in the Friday post (an Instagram Story poll or comment-count nod among that week's shortlist) for extra social engagement, but it carries no prize weight and doesn't gate the judged outcome. Keeps the whole mechanism as cheap and fast as the entry format itself — nothing here needs a dev build.
