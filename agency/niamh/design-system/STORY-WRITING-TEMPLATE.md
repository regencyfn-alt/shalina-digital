## STORY SUMMARY
*Minimum 250 words. The story, whole, in prose. No camera directions, no scene numbers — what happens, to whom, and why it matters.*

[Write the story as if telling it to someone at the table. Name the people. Name the place — the specific kitchen, field, taxi rank, classroom. Give the character a want the audience recognises in the first breath, put the day's real obstacle in front of it, and let the product enter the way it enters real life: incidentally, practically, without a speech.

Walk the arc beat by beat in plain sentences: how it opens, where it turns, what tightens, how it lands. Describe what the audience sees at the moment that matters most — the gesture, the look, the object in the hand. If there is a demonstration, describe exactly what is done and what watching it teaches. If there is a twist, earn it earlier in the text.

Close with the resolution and the brand moment: what the character has at the end that they didn't at the start, and how the locked line lands on it. The final image, described plainly.

This section is the source of truth downstream — storyboard panels, VO lines, and generation prompts are all cut from this prose. If it isn't here, it doesn't exist in production. 250 words is the floor, not the target; write what the story needs, then cut what it doesn't.]
