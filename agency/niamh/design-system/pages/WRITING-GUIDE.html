Writing Guide

How Shalina brands sound. One writing spine under five brand voices, with the claim discipline that keeps every market’s regulator satisfied and every reader respected. Companion to the Brand Guides.

v1.0 · 2026-07-07 · Niamh, Director of Art, CENTREFREE Agency

The spine — every brand, every surface

  • Evidence before claim. A named market, a real endorsement, a lived moment beats a sweeping adjective. If the proof isn’t in hand, the sentence doesn’t ship.
  • Locked lines are locked. Campaign lines and claims run verbatim — see the table below. No paraphrase, no “improvement,” no casual translation.
  • No hype-words. Cut “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “miracle,” “ultimate,” “fast-acting” (that last one is also a regulatory breach). Replace with the concrete thing.
  • Short sentences carry weight. Build to the strong line. Don’t bury it.
  • Respect the reader. We write to people in Lagos, Accra, Nairobi, Lusaka, Luanda about their own lives. We are not explaining Africa to people who live it.
  • Human truth over product truth. The product carries the clinical fact; the story carries the person. Never swap those jobs.

Voice by brand

Shal’Artem — testimony

Sincere, warm, documentary. First person, always: the person who lived it tells it. Plain and neighbourly — a neighbour recounting the day, not a brand presenting a case. Trust before sell.

Not this
“Shal’Artem’s fast-acting formula got Ada back on her feet in record time!”
This
“I bounced back in time to see my son take his first steps.”
  • Never name malaria inside a testimony; never show sickness. We enter after the turn.
  • The gold moment must be irreplaceable and specific — something that happens once and cannot be rescheduled.
  • No relief-speed language anywhere in campaign copy. The claim line, with its endorsement, does that work alone.

Shaltoux — voice-first energy

UPPERCASE display, short percussive lines, written to be said aloud. The reader’s voice is the hero — teachers, singers, traders, callers. Mood picks the register: comfort lines are warm and low; energy lines are quick; strong lines are flat and certain.

Not this
“Soothe your irritated throat with our premium lozenge experience.”
This
“BIG CLASS. LONG DAY. SOUND LIKE YOURSELF.”

Ibucap — restraint

The hardest voice because it is mostly silence. One claim exists; everything else is story. Working-life stories carry the brand — the hustle, the timing, the day that had to be finished. Copy stays out of the frame until the final beat.

Not this
“Ibucap’s triple-action formula beats headache, fever AND body pain — better than ordinary painkillers!”
This
“The Power of Three in One.” — and nothing else on the art.
  • Triple-action composition (Ibuprofen + Paracetamol + Caffeine) appears in regulator documents only — never consumer copy.
  • Heritage line is audio and end-card only, never a headline.
  • No comparatives, ever — nothing is “better,” “stronger,” or “faster than.”

ACO — documentary Portuguese

Plain, practical, proud of real work. Portuguese is the language of every final surface; English drafts are internal review artifacts and say so in the filename (-EN). Write the Portuguese as spoken Angolan Portuguese, not textbook European Portuguese.

Regulatory writing rules — hard stops

Across PPB (Kenya), NAFDAC (Nigeria) and FDA (Ghana) work: never write doctors, nurses, or pharmacists into any scene, real or animated · no medical settings · no pill-taking scenes in copy or board text unless the signed storyboard explicitly carries it (storyboard wins, per Production Law 1) · no comparative claims · no cure-speed promises beyond the locked claim lines · no dosage or composition talk in consumer copy.

When a draft trips one of these, the fix is never to soften the sentence — it is to delete the idea and tell the story another way.

Mechanics

  • Hierarchy: Eyebrow → Headline → Support, on every content block. Eyebrows are wide-tracked UPPERCASE at 0.22em.
  • Emoji: none in clinical or corporate copy; allowed in WhatsApp and social mechanics where the channel speaks that way.
  • Storyboard text becomes VO (Production Law 6): board copy is written to be spoken — one consistent voice per story, recorded in a single session. Read every board line aloud before locking it.
  • Punctuation in AI generation prompts: hyphens only — em dashes break the pipeline. (Prompt text is production material, not consumer copy, but the law rides with the writing.)
  • Numbers only when real. Budgets, market counts, endorsement bodies — all trace to the FY2026/27 plan or a signed document. No invented figures, even in drafts.
