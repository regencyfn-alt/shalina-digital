Captain Comeback's First Day

South Africa · English
16:9 hero — Lwandle at the school gate, first morning of term, the comeback arriving in his face.

The Story

The last day of the holidays, and Lwandle Mkhize is on the community court with the big boys, going for the shot that would end the afternoon a hero. He misses. The ball skims off the rim, the older ones laugh, and he walks home with that laugh following him — the kid who gets knocked down. This is the note the holiday ends on, and he carries it into the house without a word.

Evening, the Mkhize home. The new uniform hangs on the wardrobe door, shirt pressed by Zimkitha, shoes done by Sibusiso, Gogo Nomalanga passing through with a comment that isn't quite comfort and isn't quite a joke. Buhle inspects the schoolbag like a customs officer. The house is loud and ordinary and full — and nobody swoops in with a speech about tomorrow, because that is not how this house works. Lwandle lies awake with the miss still playing.

Morning. The bathroom — the permissioned space. Lwandle brushes his teeth with Flodent the way the whole family does it, unremarkable and thorough, and then he looks up into the mirror. The grin that comes back at him is a beat ahead of the rest of him: fresh, clean, ready. Something resets. Captain Comeback is in the glass. Knocked down often; never stays down.

He walks to school sharp. In the yard, the same court, the same boys, the same rim — and this time when the ball comes loose Lwandle takes it, sets, and puts it up. Whether it drops or rattles home, he has already done the braver thing: he stepped back up to the exact place that beat him. The yard sees it. The term is his to take.

Evening, home again. The family is there — the tease, the food, Gogo's verdict, Buhle's inspection of his day. Lwandle has something at the end he didn't have at the start: not a trophy, just himself, back. On his fresh grin the line lands, once.

Grab Life, Grab the Freshness!

Why this works · Brand

  1. Proves Grab Life, Grab the Freshness! without stating it — the fresh mouth is what lets him grab the day back.
  2. Anchors the morning brush as the series' permissioned space and reinforces the adult tube as the household's everyday default.
  3. Demonstrates the platform idea — brush before the brave moment — rather than repeating a slogan.
  4. Adds Lwandle's first lead to the continuity: the grown-ups and Buhle have had their moments; the comeback kid completes the pattern.
  5. Product appears real and incidental — correct adult tube, in use, no label-to-camera, claim held to the end frame.
  6. Turns the "no claim in the scene" constraint into strength: confidence shown in a look, not argued in copy — more credible, more shareable.
  7. Gives the channel plan clean cutdowns: the 10s bathroom-mirror-to-yard, the gate hero still, the WhatsApp grin-reset beat.
  8. New for the brand: a resilience arc, not a nerves-and-triumph one — the story is about returning, not winning.
  9. Earns long-arc trust — a family that lets its kids fail on camera and get back up is a family the audience believes.
  10. Commercial truth: moves the tube from grudge-buy hygiene to the small ritual that starts a good day — reason to reach for it first.

Why this works · Audience

  1. Every parent and every kid knows the walk home after the miss — they see themselves in the first three seconds.
  2. The pressed uniform on the wardrobe door is the exact texture of the night before term.
  3. The scroll stops on the laugh at the court — a small public humiliation everyone remembers in their body.
  4. At the turn — the mirror grin — they feel the reset before Lwandle does; it's a lift, not a lecture.
  5. What they learn they can use: the fresh-start ritual is real and free — brush, look up, decide the day is yours.
  6. The full-house evening, Gogo's not-quite-comfort, Buhle inspecting the bag — that's the "that's us" frame.
  7. They trust it because the story refuses to fake the win — it isn't about the shot dropping, it's about stepping back up.
  8. They send it to the one who always bounces back — a sibling, a teammate, their own kid — "this is you."
  9. A second watch rewards them: the grin arrives a beat before the boy does, and you catch it the second time.
  10. A week later they remember one image — the mirror grin — and one line, Grab Life, Grab the Freshness!
B

A Melhor Mistura — The Best Mix

Angola · Triple Action
16:9 hero — the lab table: Flodent flavours laid out, two experts mid-blend in warm light.

The Story

We open in a lab — warm and curious, not sterile. This is where the improved Flodent is made, and the film lets us watch it happen: on a clean table the flavours are laid out like ingredients that deserve getting right, and two experts work over them without hurry — combining, tasting the idea, hunting the best mix. Over it, one voice: the scientist, calm and precise, proud of the work. This is Formulação Melhorada made visible, and the scientist's voice-over is the spine of the whole film — each of the three actions named by the person who built it.

"Stronger teeth." On the scientist's first function, the film transitions home — a close on Avó Domingas, doing her job for the family, the camera panning slowly out to find her whole and easy in herself. The line and the woman meet: her strong, unbothered smile is the proof, no caption needed.

"Freshness." On the second function, the family morning. Mamã, brushing, catches the youngest in the mirror and teases the care into him — "Viste a tripla ação que te comprei?" (Did you see the triple action I got you?) Her clean, ready freshness is the thing she walks her day out on.

"Cavity protection." On the third, the kid pokes his head into frame, brush still going, grinning, and tells her exactly how much he loves it. The child, the mango, the sweets at the festa — all of it guarded, and he doesn't even know it; he just loves his toothpaste.

Three functions, one voice, one tube — the making and the loving inside the same thirty seconds. Then we exit clean into the fixed outro: the Flodent end-lockup that closes every film the same way — logo, the new pack with the 50g, and the line landing once.

Agarre a Vida, Agarre a Frescura!

Why this works · Brand

  1. Shows Formulação Melhorada being made — the lab earns the relaunch instead of asserting it; care you can see.
  2. The scientist's VO names all three functions with the authority of the maker, then the family proves each one — claim then people.
  3. Ties the triple action together with a single device (one voice, three named functions) — unmissable, never a hard sell.
  4. Establishes the Santos household — Avó, Mamã, the youngest — as Angola's first named faces (ratification pending).
  5. Product appears real: the red tube in use, the lab showing the making, pack and 50g to camera only in the fixed outro.
  6. The reusable outro guarantees brand consistency — every Flodent film ends on the identical locked lockup.
  7. Gives the channel plan clean cutdowns: the 6s lab-blend teaser, the Avó pan, the mirror beat, each carrying the VO.
  8. New for Angola: a make-it-and-love-it structure (science + family), distinct from SA's single-hero story.
  9. Earns trust for the relaunch — showing the care in the mix answers "what actually improved?" without a spoken health claim.
  10. Commercial truth: pairs a visible improvement with a family already sold on it, and names the 50g at the end frame.

Why this works · Audience

  1. The lab table is quietly fascinating — people stop to watch something being made with care.
  2. Angolan mothers recognise Avó instantly — the grandmother the whole house is built around.
  3. The scroll stops on the experts blending the flavours; it looks like care, and care is rare in a toothpaste ad.
  4. At the mother's line they feel warmth, not a pitch — "the triple action I got you" is love, spoken the way mothers speak.
  5. What they learn: the tube was improved on purpose, and the whole family is on it — one buy, everyone covered.
  6. The kid poking his head in, brush going, grinning — that is the "that's my child" frame.
  7. They trust it because the improvement is shown being made, and the maker's own voice names what it does.
  8. They send it to the family group — "look how they make the new Flodent" — exactly how Angola shares on WhatsApp.
  9. A second watch rewards them: the flavour chosen on the lab table is the one in the child's grin at the end.
  10. A week later they remember the lab table, the scientist's three words, and the line — Agarre a Vida, Agarre a Frescura!
