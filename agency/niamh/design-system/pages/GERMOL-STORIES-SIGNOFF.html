Match Day

Antiseptic Liquid · South Africa
16:9 hero — the braai in full swing, smoke and boerewors, the TV glowing inside; Mom framed between the wrecked table and the couch.

The Story

It's match day, and the braai is doing what a braai does — smoke in the air, boerewors on the fire, seven-year-old Sipho running between the relatives while everyone waits for kickoff. Then the shout from inside: "It's starting!" — and the house empties toward the television.

Everyone except Mom. She looks at the table: grease, bones, sauce, and eight pairs of filthy hands headed for her couch. She knows this maths, because she has done it every match of her life. By the time it's all clean, the anthem will be over. It always is. Sipho hesitates in the doorway, watching her stay behind. Again. That's the story's true villain — not the mess. The fact that the person who makes the moment possible is the one who always misses it.

Not this time. One basin, warm water, a capful of Germol Antiseptic Liquid — the familiar cloudy swirl blooms through the water — and she hands Sipho a cloth. "Table. You and me. Two minutes." She's gambling the anthem on doing it together rather than losing it doing everything alone. Two cloths, one table, hands rinsed in the same fresh basin, and they're moving.

The family settles onto the couch just as the anthem begins. Grandad puts his hand over his heart. Sipho copies him — then glances left. Mom is on the couch beside them. This time she didn't miss the moment she made possible, and the seven-year-old is the only one who knows what that took.

The moments that bring us together deserve a home that's ready for them. Germol Antiseptic Liquid. Shields with Love and Care.

  2. The cloudy swirl is the brand's own visual signature, deployed as the pivot of the story rather than a demo insert.
  3. One routine covers table and hands from one basin — the multi-use truth of the product carried by action, not claim.
  4. The conflict upgrade costs nothing in warmth: same braai, same family, but now something real is at stake — Mom's presence in the moment.
  5. "Shields with Love and Care" is dramatised literally: her care is the shield, Germol is how it's applied.
  6. Clean cutdowns for the channel plan: the 6s "It's starting!" table-look, the basin swirl close-up, the couch-anthem still.
  7. No hygiene fear, no germ theatre — positive-frame cleanliness, which is where the category is moving.
  8. Commercial truth: positions the bottle as the always-in-the-house staple you reach for at speed, not a special-occasion product.

  1. Every South African household knows the person who's still wiping the table when the anthem plays — and half the audience IS her.
  2. The scroll stops on a truth rarely said out loud: the maker of the moment misses the moment.

A Better End to the Day

Aloe Soothe Soap · South Africa
16:9 hero — bathroom mirror in honest evening light: her reflection, and in her hand the phone with the excuse half-typed.

The Story

After a long, hot day, a young woman gets home with just enough time to freshen up before dinner — her best friend's dinner, the kind of night you don't get a second run at. In the mirror, the day sits on her skin: shine, and a pimple on her cheek redder and more irritated than it was this morning.

And here is the real conflict — not the blemish. Her thumb, already moving: "So sorry, feeling off, don't wait for me…" The message sits on the screen, unsent. Going costs her comfort; staying costs her the night, and her friend the person who should be there. One breath held between the two.

She puts the phone face-down on the basin. Message deleted, not sent. What she reaches for instead is the routine that has never let her down: Germol Aloe Soothe. Not to fix her face — to leave the day behind before it decides her evening for her. The creamy aloe lather washes away the day's oil, sweat and impurities, gentle over the irritated cheek; cool water, pat dry. She looks again. The pimple hasn't disappeared — her skin simply looks calmer and fresher, feels soothed and comfortable. The day's shine is gone. That's enough.

Later she's laughing at the table when someone calls a selfie — and she leans in without hesitation. She's inside the moment instead of inside the mirror. And the message she almost sent stays unsent forever.

Some days show on your skin. Gentle care helps you leave the day behind. Germol Aloe Soothe. Shields with Love and Care.

Why this works · Brand

  1. Fully compliant by design: the soap cleanses and soothes — cosmetic language only. The blemish is still visible at the payoff; it simply looks calmer. No treatment claim anywhere in the film.
  2. The honesty IS the differentiation — in a category of airbrushed-skin promises, a brand that shows the pimple at the happy ending is the one that gets believed.
  3. Gentle-care positioning is dramatised as strength, not weakness: gentleness is what makes the night possible.
  4. The unsent message gives the story a true antagonist without inventing a skin miracle to defeat it.
  5. The product's role is precise: it removes the day, and removing the day is what returns her confidence — the causality flatters the brand without overclaiming.
  6. Clean cutdowns: the 6s thumb-typing beat, the phone-face-down decision, the lean-into-the-selfie close.
  7. Builds the evening-routine occasion — daily use, not crisis use.
  8. Commercial truth: recruits the young-adult buyer to a brand she may still think of as her mother's, through her own Tuesday-night reality.

Why this works · Audience

  1. Everyone has typed that excuse message. Half of them have sent it. The scroll stops on the guilt of recognition.
  2. The stakes are social and real — a best friend's night — not cosmetic vanity, which makes her choice admirable rather than shallow.
  3. The decision beat is a gesture, not a speech: phone face-down. Audiences quote gestures.
  4. Young women are exhausted by "flawless" advertising — a calmer-not-cured mirror shot reads as respect.
  5. The selfie payoff is the exact currency of the audience's own evenings.
  6. What they learn they can use tonight: wash the day off before you decide anything about the evening.
  7. They send it to the friend who always almost-cancels — "come anyway."
  8. A week later they remember the phone going face-down, and the line: leave the day behind.

Right on Time

Charcoal Soap · South Africa
16:9 hero — the rear-view mirror: grease to the collar, dust in his hairline, brake lights to the horizon, the dashboard clock against six.

The Story

The workshop closes and a young mechanic climbs into his bakkie as a voice note comes through from his wife: "Emma's been asking all day if you'll make it. The concert starts at six." He smiles. "I'll be there." Then the road disagrees.

Emma's song is the opening item — six sharp, no buffer — and traffic takes back twelve minutes he doesn't have. In the rear-view mirror the whole day looks back at him: grease to the collar, dust in his hairline, tired eyes. So the choice is real, and both arms of it cost: walk in on time looking like the workshop, or stop to wash and risk walking in on an empty stage. There is no version of this where he loses nothing.

He chooses Dad over punctual. Through the front door — "Daddy! Hurry!" — and he answers "Give me two minutes" out loud, like a promise, tap already running, Germol Charcoal Soap in hand. Hands first, then face and neck; the rich charcoal lather lifts the day's dust, sweat and grime, and the dark water carries the workshop away. Clean shirt. The flowers off the table. Go.

He slips into the row just as the lights go down — not comfortably early, but there. Emma finds him from the stage mid-song: clean shirt, flowers on his lap. Afterwards she runs straight to him, and the nearness of the miss is what makes her face worth the whole film.

A hard day's work deserves an even better evening. Leave the Day Behind. Germol Charcoal Soap. Shields with Love and Care.

Why this works · Brand

  1. "Leave the Day Behind" is made literal and visible — the day rinses off dark, on camera, in the product's own signature texture.
  2. The two minutes of washing are the dramatic engine, not a hygiene insert: the lather has to work inside the time the choice bought.
  3. A genuine dilemma with a cost on both arms is the strongest conflict in the set — and the product is the bridge, not the hero.
  4. Charcoal's masculine-grooming credibility is earned through a working man, not a gym mirror.
  5. The near-miss protects honesty: Germol doesn't make him early, it makes him HIM in time.
  6. Clean cutdowns: the 6s rear-view look, the dark-water rinse close-up, the lights-down slip into the row.
  7. Builds the end-of-workday occasion for daily use, five days a week.
  8. Commercial truth: gives hardworking men permission to see soap choice as part of showing up for family — a reason to buy that isn't vanity.

Why this works · Audience

  1. Every working parent has done the maths in that rear-view mirror. The scroll stops on the grease and the clock together.
  2. "No version where he loses nothing" is the texture of real life — audiences trust stories that admit it.
  3. The voice note is exactly how these messages arrive — the film sounds like their phone.
  4. "Give me two minutes," shouted through a bathroom door, is every dad in the country.
  5. Emma scanning the audience is a universal held breath — they feel the empty seat before he fills it.
  6. The flowers waiting on the table reward a second watch: he'd planned to be Dad all along; the day just tried to stop him.
  7. They send it to the father who never misses a concert — or the one who did, once, and still talks about it.
  8. A week later they remember the dark water rinsing away, and Emma spotting him mid-song.

Movie Night

Original Soap · South Africa
16:9 hero — she leans against the closed front door in uniform, eyes shut; around the corner, the blanket fort glows and the popcorn bowl waits.

The Story

A nurse checks on her last patient — "Thank you, Sister" — and quietly ends a twelve-hour shift of caring for everyone else. She comes home, closes the front door, and leans against it with the whole ward still on her skin.

Her young son peeks around the corner. The blanket fort is already built. The popcorn bowl has been ready since five. "Mom… we're still having movie night… right?" And for one honest second — the second every parent watching will recognise — the sentence in her mouth is "Not tonight, my love." His hopeful face is already reading hers. That second is the whole conflict, and it's bigger than any villain.

She doesn't say it. "Ten minutes," she says instead — and the bath she runs isn't a freshen-up, it's the hinge of the evening. Germol Original Soap; the rich creamy lather gently cleansing away the day's sweat and impurities while caring for her skin; the ward washed off; the shift ended on purpose. This is how she changes shifts — from Sister to Mom. Comfortable clothes, damp hair, shoulders finally down, she walks into the lounge arriving home a second time — this time all the way.

"There you are." They settle in. Halfway through the movie he curls up and rests his head on her shoulder; he's asleep before the credits. She stays to the end anyway. After a day spent caring for everyone else, she finally gets to just be Mom — and she's not wasting a minute of it, even the minutes he sleeps through.

After a day spent caring for others, come home to the care you trust. Germol Original Soap. Shields with Love and Care.
