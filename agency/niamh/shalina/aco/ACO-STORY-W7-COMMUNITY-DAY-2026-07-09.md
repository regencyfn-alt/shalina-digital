## STORY SUMMARY

Sunday, mid-morning, Glória's flat — the community gathering is hers to host this month, the kind of day that used to mean her own small hurricane the night before. We open on her already mid-clean, alone, no Ana in frame. She reaches under the sink without looking, the way a hand finds a light switch in a dark room it knows well: one tub, one bucket, one capful. She doesn't check the label. She doesn't hesitate over which product goes where. The camera holds on the unremarkable fact of it — a woman doing an ordinary Sunday chore, except the extraordinary thing is what's *missing*: no five bottles fanned across the counter, no swapping products room to room, no small hurricane.

Ana arrives early to help carry chairs, lets herself in, and stops in the doorway a beat too long. Glória is already three rooms deep, bucket in hand, humming. Ana doesn't say anything about it — she just clocks it, the way you'd clock a friend who's quit a habit without announcing it: a small private smile, no dialogue needed. She picks up a chair and joins in, and for a moment they work in the same rhythm they haven't shared since W6's list.

Guests begin arriving as the last surface dries. Someone compliments the smell in passing, the way people always do now — Glória barely registers it, waves it off mid-conversation, already moved on to greeting the next arrival. It isn't a moment for her anymore. That's the whole point.

Final image: the ACO tub, unremarkable, back under the sink where it lives — not displayed, not staged, just *there*, the way a fixture is there. **You Only Need One.**

1. Closes the Proof arc on the strongest claim a cleaning brand can make: not "it works," not "she proved it," but "she doesn't think about it anymore" — habit is the highest form of trust.

3. Ana's role shrinks on purpose: she's a witness, not a coach, this time — signalling the method has left her hands and become Glória's own, which is the exact behaviour change row the content plan asks for.
4. Series continuity lands its full arc: W4 admiration → W5 pressure → W6 trial → W7 unconscious habit — each film removed one prop (the coaching, the stopwatch, the list) until only the reaching-hand gesture remains.
5. Pack law kept understated by design: the tub appears reached-for, not displayed — the final "back under the sink" image is the first time in the series the product is deliberately *not* the hero shot, which is itself the argument.
6. Sets up W8 (Neighbourhood Gossip) precisely: guests noticing the smell "in passing" here becomes the seed of the wider social-proof story next episode.
7. Documentary discipline rewarded: a real hosting morning, real chairs, real doorbell of guests arriving in a stream rather than one dramatic entrance — banks credibility for the Conversion phase's bigger emotional swings.
8. Feeds the channel plan cleanly: the reaching-hand cold open as a 6s TikTok hook, the Ana-in-the-doorway smile as a WhatsApp status still, the "unremarkable tub under the sink" as the companion static's visual anchor.
9. Commercially, this is the film for the viewer who's already tried it once (post-W6 UGC) — it tells them what the product looks like a month in: not a project, a fixture.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. Hosting-day dread is close to universal — the film opens on the exact morning viewers recognise, then subverts the panic they expect.
2. The reaching-hand-without-looking gesture is a small, true detail of real habit formation — anyone who's ever stopped thinking about a chore they used to dread will feel it before they can name why.
3. Scroll-stop: Ana standing in the doorway a beat too long, saying nothing — a pause where the audience expects dialogue is a pause that makes them lean in.
4. The turn is emotional, not mechanical this time: it's a friendship beat (quiet recognition, no words) rather than a product demo — a register change that keeps the series from repeating itself.
5. What they take away is aspirational in the gentlest way: not "buy this and prove something," but "one day this stops being effort" — a believable, low-pressure promise.
6. "That's us" moment: the guest who compliments the smell "in passing" and gets waved off mid-sentence — recognisable as the exact way real compliments land on a busy hosting day.
7. Rewards the loyal viewer specifically: only someone who watched W6 will register that this is the same list-and-bucket ritual, now invisible — new viewers still read it as a simple, warm morning.
8. Shareability: this is the film people send captioned "this is the goal" rather than "this is us" — an aspirational share rather than a recognition share, a new register for the series.
9. Second-watch reward: counting how many rooms Glória clears before Ana even says a word — the silence is doing the work the dialogue did in W5 and W6.
10. A week later they remember one image: a tub, unremarkable, back under the sink where it lives. **Join the shift.**
