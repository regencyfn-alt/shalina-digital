## STORY SUMMARY

Glória's phone buzzes on the kitchen counter: her husband's cousins are twenty minutes out, unannounced, three of them, and one is the aunt who inspects skirting boards with a finger. The old Glória would already be a small hurricane — five bottles out, none of them finished, no plan. Instead she does what she has started doing since W3 and W4: she calls Ana.

Ana arrives with nothing but the ACO tub under one arm, and the two of them move through Glória's flat like it's a drill they've rehearsed, because in a sense the film has been building it since the Grease Test. One measure into one bucket at the sink. The bucket travels: the front hallway tiles where the aunt will first stand, the kitchen counter still carrying breakfast, the bathroom mirror flecked with toothpaste, the good-room floor. No wasted trips back to a cupboard — because the cupboard has nothing else to fetch. Quick, real flashes: Glória wiping the hallway on her knees, laughing at herself; Ana rinsing the bucket at the sink between rooms; the pine scent standing in for a montage the film doesn't need to show twice.

The doorbell goes with ninety seconds to spare by the visible kitchen clock. Glória freezes for one beat — old instinct — then looks around at a flat that is simply, believably clean. She opens the door composed. The aunt runs her finger along the skirting board out of habit, finds nothing, and instead compliments the smell. Glória and Ana trade one look over the guests' shoulders: the look of two people who both know exactly why this worked and aren't going to explain it to anyone standing at the door.

Final image: the ACO tub back on the counter, guests seated, Glória still faintly out of breath but smiling. **You Only Need One.**

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Converts W4's admiration into action: Glória doesn't just watch the routine, she performs it herself with Ana coaching — the viewer's own hands are the next logical step.
2. The one-bucket, no-return-trips mechanic gets its second outing in a genuinely different home layout — proof the method isn't staged for one house.
3. Time pressure (twenty minutes, ninety seconds to spare) turns the product claim into a stopwatch the audience can believe, without a single on-screen number or graphic.
4. Series continuity: W3 saw it work → W4 saw it as a life → W5 Glória does it herself under real pressure → W6 she issues the direct challenge. Each film raises her stake by exactly one notch.
5. Pack law kept: one real tub, no re-lettering, incidental placement (under Ana's arm on arrival, back on the counter at the close).
6. The aunt-with-the-inspecting-finger is a culturally specific, instantly recognisable stock character — she does the "quality test" so the ad never has to state a claim aloud.
7. Documentary discipline holds: real clutter, a real doorbell countdown, no supernatural shine — credibility banked for the Conversion-phase films that follow.
8. Feeds the channel plan: three clean cutdowns (the phone-buzz cold open, the travelling-bucket hallway-to-bathroom sequence, the door-open payoff) plus a WhatsApp status still of the final look between the two women.
9. Commercially, this is the film that turns a warm/curious viewer into someone who tries it before their *next* unannounced guest — the CTA payoff is immediate, not aspirational.
10. Sets up W6 cleanly: the challenge Glória issues next episode is one she has now already half-answered herself.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. The unannounced-relatives cold open is a near-universal fear; the twenty-minute countdown puts the viewer's own pulse into the first three seconds.
2. The "aunt who inspects skirting boards" is a specific, shared joke — recognition laughter opens the door for the product message that follows.
3. Scroll-stop: Glória calling a friend for help instead of panicking alone — an emotionally true, slightly vulnerable beat rarely shown in cleaning ads.
4. The turn is satisfying because it's earned, not magic: we see the actual bucket travel actual rooms in real time, not a jump-cut to "clean."
5. What they learn is usable in the next five minutes of their own life: one bucket, no return trips, start at the door guests will see first.
6. "That's us" moment: the freeze at the doorbell, old instinct fighting new habit, before she opens the door composed anyway.
7. The payoff look between Ana and Glória — not explained to the guests — rewards viewers who've followed the series; new viewers still get the joke from context alone.
8. Shareability: this is the film friends send each other captioned "this is us before your mum visits."
9. Second-watch reward: the visible kitchen clock counting down: a real, checkable detail that holds up under a rewatch.
10. A week later they remember the stopwatch feeling and the line: readiness that isn't luck. **Be ready anytime.**
