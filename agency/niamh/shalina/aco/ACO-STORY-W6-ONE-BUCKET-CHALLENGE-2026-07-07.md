## STORY SUMMARY

Saturday morning, Glória's own house — her territory, her rules, and that matters: this time she is not visiting Ana's calm, she is testing it where the mess is hers. She has been fair-minded about it and thorough. On the kitchen table she has laid out the challenge like a referee: one bucket, one jar of ACO — and beside them, taped to the table, a hand-written list: KITCHEN. BATHROOM. TILES. FLOORS. DINING. Five surfaces, one product, no substitutions. Her arms fold. The whole street knows that fold. *"You keep saying it. Prove it."*

Ana doesn't argue. She rolls her sleeves, measures one capful of ACO into the bucket — the camera stays honest on the dilution, because the mechanic is the message — and the water clouds pine-green. Then the run, room by room, Glória following as inspector with her list and a pen. The kitchen: grease by the stove, the counter where the oil tin sits, done, ticked — reluctantly. The bathroom: sink and tub, soap-scum ring lifted in two passes, ticked. The wall tiles by the back door, where every household's hands land: wiped bright, ticked. The floors, corridor to front room, the big surface, the one Glória privately believed would beat one bucket: the water still working, the mop still pulling dirt, ticked. Finally the dining room — the good table, the one nobody experiments on. Glória hovers. Ana wrings the cloth, wipes, and steps back. Glória runs her own finger along the wood, looks at her finger, looks at Ana — and ticks the last line slowly, like a judge signing something she can't appeal.

Same bucket. Same water. Five ticks. Glória holds up the list to the camera herself — the sceptic publishing her own verdict — and almost, almost smiles. Ana just lifts the jar beside her.

Final image: the finished list and the ACO tub side by side on the good table. **You Only Need One.**

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Proves the locked line by *contract*: terms named on camera, five surfaces, one dilution — the claim is tested at its hardest and passes on the record.
2. Formalises the campaign's core mechanic — one capful, one bucket, whole house — as a repeatable ritual anyone can copy tomorrow.
3. The platform idea is demonstrated, not repeated: nobody says "multipurpose"; a hand-written list and five ticks say it beyond argument.
4. Series continuity pays off: three weeks of Glória's scepticism spend themselves here — and her conversion is earned, which protects the Conversion phase from feeling scripted.
5. The pack appears working, real, correct: one tub, real packshot, dilution shown honestly — brand law kept where a lesser ad would fake concentration.
6. Documentary constraint as strength: the sceptic runs the test in her own house, with her own mess — the film's credibility engine is the very rule that forbids staging.
7. Channel plan fed richly: five natural surface clips for cutdowns, the list as a static ("Five Surfaces Tested" companion piece is already in the plan), the finger-swipe as a 6s TikTok hook.
8. New for the brand since W4/W5: the audience graduates from watching Ana's life to a protocol they can run — passive proof becomes participatory proof.
9. Long-arc earning: Glória's self-published verdict (holding the list to camera) is the social-proof template the Neighbourhood Gossip and finale stories will scale.
10. Commercial truth: the challenge converts the convinced-but-inert viewer — it replaces "I believe it works" with "I know exactly what to buy and what to do with it," one tub deep.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. Anyone who has ever said *"prove it"* to an advert is standing in Glória's shoes from the first frame — the sceptic is their representative, not the brand's puppet.
2. The hand-written list taped to the table is the lived truth: this is how real households settle real arguments — terms first, in ink.
3. Scroll-stop: the arms folding over the five-item list at second three — a challenge thrown is a promise of a verdict, and nobody leaves before a verdict.
4. At the turn — the finger-swipe on the good table — they feel the sceptic's own suspense: *this* is the surface where the trick should fail.
5. What they learn is exact and free: one capful per bucket, the order of rooms, the same water lasting the run — a protocol, not a slogan.
6. "That's us": the dining table nobody is allowed to experiment on, and the neighbour whose fold of the arms the whole street knows.
7. They trust it because the film gives the sceptic the pen: Glória ticks her own list, runs her own finger-test, and publishes her own verdict to camera.
8. They send it with a dare attached — to the friend, sister, or husband who doubts: "watch her list. Then tell me." The film is built to be forwarded as an argument-ender.
9. Second watch rewards: the pen hesitating at each tick, the bucket water darkening honestly as it works, Ana never once looking worried.
10. A week later they remember one image and one line: five ticks on a hand-written list, and *You Only Need One.*
