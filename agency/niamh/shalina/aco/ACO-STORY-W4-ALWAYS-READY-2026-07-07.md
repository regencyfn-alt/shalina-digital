## STORY SUMMARY

Mid-morning, Luanda, a weekday. Glória arrives at Ana's gate unannounced — she has a reason ready (returning a dish), but the real reason walks in with her: since the grease test she has been watching her friend the way you watch someone who knows something you don't. And today is exactly the wrong day to visit anyone. Ana's morning is the kind that eats women alive: her sister has phoned to say she's bringing the church committee past at noon, the twins have tracked mud down the corridor, and there is a pot on the stove that has boiled over onto the enamel.

Glória stands in the doorway waiting for the panic she knows from her own house. It doesn't come. Ana laughs at the corridor, hands Glória a coffee, and moves through the morning like water: one jar comes down from the shelf — the ACO tub, the only bottle in the cupboard — one measure into one bucket, and the same bucket travels. Corridor tiles, done, mud gone. The stovetop, the overboil lifting in one pass. A cloth wrung out for the bathroom sink while she's passing it anyway. There is no drama in any of it and that is the point: quick flashes, real hands, real surfaces, the pine smell you can almost hear. Between rooms she is still in the conversation — asking about Glória's mother, laughing at the dish she brought back — because the cleaning is not a project. It is one gesture repeated.

At last Glória asks the question the audience has been asking with her: *"How are you always ready? Always?"* Ana doesn't make a speech. She turns the tub so the label faces her friend and says the truth of the whole campaign in one breath: no juggling ten products, no complicated routine — one jar, for everything, so there is nothing to think about. Less stress. More control. The committee's knock lands at noon on a spotless, calm house, and Glória watches Ana open the door unhurried — then looks back at the single tub on the counter.

Final image: the ACO tub on the clean counter, morning light, the line over it. **You Only Need One.**

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Proves "You Only Need One" as a *lifestyle claim*, not a product demo — the line is never spoken as advertising, it is lived for 40 seconds and then shown.
2. Introduces the travelling-bucket ritual — one measure, one bucket, every room — the usage mechanic that W6 will formalise as the Challenge.
3. Demonstrates the platform idea (fewer products = less stress = more control) through pacing itself: Ana's calm IS the argument.
4. Series continuity: Glória's watching-and-wondering arc advances one honest step — seen it work (W3) → seen it as a life (W4) → will demand proof (W6).
5. The pack appears incidentally and correctly: real packshot, one tub, the only bottle in the cupboard — brand law kept, and the empty cupboard is itself the claim.
6. Documentary constraint turned strength: no re-lettered packs, no staged sparkle — real surfaces and real hands make the proof credible in a market suspicious of miracle ads.
7. Channel plan fed: three natural cutdowns (corridor 6s, stovetop 6s, the question-and-answer 15s) plus the final lockup as a WhatsApp status still.
8. New for the brand since W3: multi-surface breadth in one story — Discovery proved depth on one stain; Proof now shows width across the home.
9. Long-arc earning: Ana answers a friend's sincere question rather than pitching — trust banked for the Conversion phase, where neighbours start asking the same thing.
10. Commercial truth: the story moves the mid-funnel — the viewer who believed the grease test now sees the routine she could adopt tomorrow with one purchase.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. The woman with the impossible mid-morning — committee coming, mud in the corridor, pot boiled over — is the viewer, within three seconds.
2. The unannounced visit with a pretext (the returned dish) is exactly how these visits happen; the social truth is right before the product ever appears.
3. Scroll-stop: Ana *laughing* at the muddy corridor — the reaction that shouldn't happen — plus the single tub coming down from an otherwise bare shelf.
4. At the turn they feel named envy — the good kind: not of Ana's house, but of her calm. Admiration with a question attached.
5. What they learn is usable tonight: one measure, one bucket, the bucket travels. No new equipment, no new routine to memorise.
6. "That's us" moment: still holding the conversation while wiping the sink she was passing anyway — cleaning woven into life, not carved out of it.
7. They trust it because the film refuses to fake: the mud is real mud, the overboil is a real overboil, nothing gleams supernaturally, and Ana never speeches.
8. They send it to the friend whose cupboard looks like Glória's — with the message the film hands them ready-made: "how are you always ready?"
9. Second watch rewards: the cupboard glimpsed with one tub in it early; the dish Glória returns as her pretext; the clock ticking toward noon in the background.
10. A week later they remember one image and one line: the bucket travelling from room to room, and *You Only Need One.*
