Why Are You Always Ready?

Working PT: Porque Estás Sempre Pronta? — Elvis's pass before anything ships

Two homes, one afternoon, cut against each other. The whole film is a contrast of time: while Glória is still cleaning, Ana is already living.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–6sSplit rhythm opens. Glória scrubs the kitchen with one product; hard cut — Ana, done, teaching her kids at the table.
  2. 6–14sGlória moves to the bathroom, another bottle; cut — Ana on the sofa, feet up, her soap opera on.
  3. 14–22sGlória works through the cupboard, still going; cut — Ana already finished, unhurried.
  4. 22–28sThey meet. Glória, half-laughing: how do you find the time for all of this? Ana lifts one tub — one diluted bowl, the whole home.
  5. 28–30sEnd card. The hours handed back.
TruthFewer products = time
EmotionAdmiration / curiosity
CTADiscover the ACO Advantage

Why it works: the intercut makes the benefit visible instead of stated — the audience counts the time gap themselves, so the claim is theirs, not ours.

RFE · ACO · Arc 02Primary · with Tshwari

The One Bucket Challenge

Working PT: O Desafio do Balde — Elvis's pass; developed with Tshwari

A mechanic, not a monologue. One bucket, one measure, one home — Ana dares Glória to clean the entire flat from a single diluted bucket, and Glória takes the bet.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–5sChallenge framed to camera / to Glória: everything you need for the whole home is in this one bucket. One bucket. Prove me wrong.
  2. 5–10sThe measure, named aloud and shown: the diluted bowl mixed once. This is the only thing that refills.
  3. 10–24sMontage against a soft clock — kitchen, bathroom, floor, glass — same bucket, room after room, no second product ever enters frame.
  4. 24–30sBucket set down, still one. Glória concedes with a grin. End card + challenge prompt to the audience.
TruthOne dilution does it all
EmotionPlayful proof
CTATake the One Bucket Challenge

Why it works: a named, repeatable challenge is a Phase-3 UGC engine — the film hands social teams a format real users can film themselves, so the ad becomes a template.

RFE · ACO · Arc 03Primary · Air 21 Jul

Unexpected Guests

Working PT: Visitas Inesperadas — Elvis's pass before anything ships

The clock starts on a phone call. Guests in twenty minutes; Glória panics and grabs product after product. Ana arrives calm and teaches the fast way — she doesn't rescue, she shows.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–6sThe call. Twenty minutes. Glória's face; the pile of bottles she reaches for; rising stress.
  2. 6–12sAna steps in, unhurried. Mixes the ready-bowl on screen and names the measure aloud: two teaspoons of ACO in one litre of water.
  3. 12–24sOne solution, carried room to room — kitchen counter, bathroom, floors. The panic drains out of the frame as she goes.
  4. 24–30sDoorbell. Home is ready; Glória is ready. End card.
TruthSpeed + versatility
EmotionStress → relief
CTABe Ready Anytime

Why it works: teaching the dilution — not doing it for her — is the exact beat that moves Glória a step closer to switching; the audience learns the measure they can use tonight.

RFE · ACO · Arc 04Primary · Conversion

The Cupboard Clean-Out

Working PT: A Arrumação do Armário — Elvis's pass before anything ships

Glória, now converted, opens the cupboard crammed with half-used bottles and, one by one, lets them go — until a single tub is all that's left, and all that's needed.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–7sThe overloaded cupboard: eight bottles, sticky rings, chaos. Glória surveys it with new eyes.
  2. 7–20sThe clear-out rhythm — bottle, bottle, bottle into the crate. Each one a small relief. Space opens on the shelf.
  3. 20–27sWhat remains: one ACO tub, alone, centred. The shelf breathes. She leaves the door open — nothing to hide.
  4. 27–30sEnd card. Room made for one.
TruthOne replaces the shelf
EmotionRelief / liberation
CTAMake Room for One

Why it works: the physical act of removal dramatises money and space saved without a single cost claim — the empty shelf is the argument, and it's undeniable.

RFE · ACO · Arc 05Primary · Conversion

The Maths on the Counter

Working PT: As Contas no Balcão — Elvis's pass before anything ships

No lecture — just arithmetic you can see. The many bottles line up on one side of the counter; one ACO tub on the other. The counter does the talking.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–8sHands set down the usual haul — floor cleaner, glass, bathroom, kitchen — a row of separate bottles across the counter.
  2. 8–18sBeside them, one tub placed down. The visual weight tips; the row suddenly looks like a lot for what one jar covers.
  3. 18–27sQuick cuts: the one tub doing each job the row promised — same result, one purchase.
  4. 27–30sEnd card. Do the maths.
TruthCheaper per clean
EmotionQuiet realisation
CTADo the Maths

Why it works: a value film that never states a price — the composition itself carries the cost argument, which keeps it credible and market-flexible.

RFE · ACO · Arc 06Primary · Loyalty

Pass It On

Working PT: Passa a Palavra — Elvis's pass before anything ships

The convert becomes the teacher. Glória shows a newcomer the one gesture — the diluted bowl — and the ritual moves one household further, the way real habits actually spread.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–7sA newcomer's kitchen, first day in a new place, surrounded by too many products she bought out of habit.
  2. 7–18sGlória, unhurried, mixes the bowl and names the measure — the same lesson Ana once gave her, now in her hands.
  3. 18–27sThe newcomer tries it; it works; the surplus bottles get quietly set aside. A knowing look between them.
  4. 27–30sEnd card. Pass it on.
TruthSimple enough to teach
EmotionWarmth / pride
CTAPass It On

Why it works: closes the funnel by making advocacy the story — the film models the exact word-of-mouth behaviour the brand wants, and a taught gesture is the most shareable asset in the set.

RFE · ACO · Arc 07Cutting Floor

The Sceptic

Working PT: O Céptico — spare option; tone check needed

Someone in the household insists one product can't possibly do the bathroom and the kitchen. The film proves them wrong across the cuts — gently, and without a word of triumph.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–7sThe doubt, said out loud: one bottle for everything? That's marketing. Arms crossed.
  2. 10–24sAna simply proceeds — glass, tile, floor, greasy hob — the same tub, cut to cut, no argument offered.
  3. 24–30sThe sceptic runs a finger along a clean surface, out of things to say. Small nod. End card.
TruthVersatility silences doubt
EmotionWry vindication
CTASee for Yourself

Why it's on the floor: strong hook, but the "doubter" register risks tipping combative — keep as a spare unless we want an edge. Cast of the sceptic waits on ratification.

RFE · ACO · Arc 08Cutting Floor

Market Day

Working PT: Dia de Mercado — spare option; may overlap Shopping Trip

Ana walks the market. Everyone else loads a trolley of cleaning products; she passes the whole aisle with a small, certain smile and one tub in the basket.

The thirty seconds

  1. 0–8sBusy market rhythm; baskets and trolleys filling with bottle after bottle.
  2. 8–20sAna moves through it, unbothered, reaching past the crowded shelf for a single tub — one reach, done.
  3. 20–27sAt the till her basket is almost empty next to the overflowing ones. The certain smile.
  4. 27–30sEnd card. One is enough.
TruthOne purchase, not a trolley
EmotionConfident simplicity
CTAOne Is Enough

Why it's on the floor: clean idea but treads close to the Shopping Trip (W7) beat — hold as an alternate cut or a stills-only mechanic rather than a second market film.

Holds & House Rules — before anything ships

PT is held. Every title and line above is a working English draft. Portuguese titles and dialogue wait on Elvis's POR pass — nothing is lettered until he rules.

Cast is held. Only Ana and Glória are established. Newcomers, sceptic and market faces stay unnamed until the Angola family cast ratification.

Packshot law. The ACO tub is only ever the real pack — the reference media is the label. It is never described, re-lettered or invented in any downstream prompt.

No visuals here, by design. These are treatments — the source of truth. Storyboards, VO and any generation are cut from the story, not around it, and only on your go.
