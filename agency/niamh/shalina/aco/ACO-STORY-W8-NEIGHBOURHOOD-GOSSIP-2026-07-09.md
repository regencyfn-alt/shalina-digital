## STORY SUMMARY

Late afternoon, the shared courtyard — washing lines, plastic chairs, the hour when the day's work loosens into talk. Two neighbours fold laundry and trade the question that has apparently been circulating since Glória's community day: how are *both* those homes always ready? A third leans in from a doorway with her theory — they must spend a fortune, you've seen the imported bottles people bring back. A fourth is certain it's a cleaning lady, twice a week, minimum. Nobody's asking meanly; it's the affectionate arithmetic of a street that notices everything. The theories get more elaborate the longer nobody checks them.

Glória crosses the courtyard mid-speculation with her shopping — and the talk does what talk does: goes quiet a half-beat too late. She's heard the tail of it. A beat. Then, instead of embarrassment, amusement: she was the twenty-bottle theory once, and she knows exactly how this ends. She waves the nearest neighbour over — *come, I'll show you* — and we cut inside to the cupboard from W7: one tub, unremarkable, where it lives. No speech. She just taps the lid, the way you'd settle a bet.

The last movement is the ripple: the same neighbours who were trading theories are now trading the answer — over the wall, at the water tap, in a WhatsApp voice note played half to camera. The question hasn't stopped moving through the neighbourhood; it's just changed direction. Somewhere behind it, Ana passes with her own bags and catches Glória's eye: the smallest possible acknowledgement between the first believer and the newest evangelist.

Final image: the tub on the cupboard shelf, door left open this time — nothing hidden, nothing to hide. **You Only Need One.**

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Opens Conversion on the mechanism Conversion actually runs on: not another demo, but other people talking — the film dramatises word-of-mouth instead of asking for it.
2. The reveal is delivered by Glória, not Ana — the recent convert carries more persuasive weight than the originator, and it proves the W7 claim that the method has fully left Ana's hands.
3. The speculation beats do covert value work: "they must spend a fortune" and "it must be professionals" are the two real-world objections to a spotless home, and the one-jar reveal answers both in a single image — cheaper than the theories, simpler than the theories.
4. Pack law escalates correctly: after W7 deliberately un-heroed the tub, W8 makes it the answer to a publicly asked question — the product's biggest moment in the series so far, earned by three weeks of not forcing it.
5. Series geometry pays off: the camera leaving the two flats for the courtyard mirrors the claim itself leaving the two households for the neighbourhood — form and message in the same move.
6. Sets up W9 (Cupboard Clean-Out) directly: the neighbours now asking "where do I get it" is the social pressure under which Glória finally clears the old bottles — and W10's finale flashbacks gain a public witness layer.
7. Documentary discipline holds under an ensemble: courtyard, washing lines, water tap, voice notes — the gossip infrastructure of a real street, observed not invented, which keeps affectionate comedy from tipping into caricature.
8. The affection is strategic: the street is never mocked for speculating — they're proven *right to have noticed* — so the audience who gossips exactly like this feels seen, not judged.
9. Channel-native by construction: the whispered-theory cold open is a 6s TikTok hook; the tap-on-the-lid reveal is the WhatsApp status still; the voice-note beat hands social teams a format to replicate with real users in Phase 3 UGC.
10. Commercially this is the widest-net film yet: W4–W7 spoke to the viewer cleaning her own home; W8 speaks to everyone who's ever wondered about someone else's — a strictly larger audience, met exactly at Conversion.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. Neighbourhood speculation about a spotless home is a universally recognised social ritual — the film opens inside a conversation the audience has personally had, on both ends.
2. The theories are the audience's own theories: expensive imports or hired help are what viewers genuinely assume — so the reveal corrects *them*, gently, not a fictional strawman.
3. Scroll-stop: the talk going quiet a half-beat too late when Glória crosses — everyone has been on both sides of that half-beat, and it lands as comedy and recognition at once.
4. Glória's amusement instead of embarrassment is the emotional turn: the audience braces for an awkward-caught moment and gets confidence — the shift itself demonstrates what owning the answer feels like.
5. "Come, I'll show you" is the most imitable gesture in the series so far — no list, no demo, just opening a cupboard — and imitable is exactly what a Conversion film wants to be.
6. "That's us" moment: the WhatsApp voice note played aloud, half to camera — the way answers actually travel through a bairro now, and the first time the series shows its own distribution channel inside the story.
7. Rewards the loyal viewer: the cupboard, the tub's shelf position, and Ana-and-Glória's wordless eye contact all carry meaning only W4–W7 viewers can read — while a first-time viewer still gets a complete mystery-and-reveal in 40 seconds.
8. Shareability is literal: this is a film about the exact act of sharing it — sending it captioned "é isto que eu dizia" *is* the plot; the share button finishes the story.
9. Second-watch reward: every theory floated in the first ten seconds is visibly disproven by something in the final ten — the fortune (one jar), the professionals (Glória's own hands, W7), the mystery (an open cupboard door).
10. A week later they remember one gesture: a woman tapping the lid of a tub like settling a bet. **Find out why.**
