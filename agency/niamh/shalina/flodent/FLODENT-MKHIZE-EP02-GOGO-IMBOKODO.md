## THE STORY

It is Women's Month, and Gogo Nomalanga is up before the block that needs her. She brushes with Flodent at the mirror - not for courage, she has never been short of that, but because a woman does not step into her day with less than her best smile - and then she is out, straight into the first fire: the clinic queue is chaos and an elder is about to be turned away before the doors close. The clock is against her. Gogo reorders the whole queue with a smile and one true word nobody else would say, and the elder is seen.

Word travels fast, and by midday the street brings her everything - including the thing nobody wants to touch: a young one in real trouble, a dispute about to boil over. She has until the day turns to cool it. No filter, all sun, she lands the truth wrapped in that smile, and the block moves because she moved through it. She is the rock the proverb means - wathint' imbokodo, you strike a woman, you strike a rock.

Evening on the stoep, she holds court, telling the day back bigger than it was, and the family teases her without mercy and loves her without limit.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

Everyone knows a Gogo like this - the matriarch who runs more of the neighbourhood than any office ever could. She is funny because she is fearless, and moving because her strength is offered as care. Imbokodo is the exact cultural note of Women's Month in South Africa, and putting a 67-year-old woman at the centre of a fast, joyful day is the kind of representation the feed rewards and shares.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

Gogo's smile is her instrument - the thing that lets her say hard truths softly and get the car moved, the queue fixed, the young one fed. Flodent is what keeps that instrument sharp at 67. The brand sits inside a life of competence and community, exactly the Fresh Start pillar: families with values who flourish and put community first. She is the most quotable face in the cast; her episodes travel.
