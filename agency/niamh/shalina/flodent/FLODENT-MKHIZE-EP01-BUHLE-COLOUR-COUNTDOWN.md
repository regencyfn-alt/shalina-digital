## THE STORY

It is show-and-tell morning, and Buhle Mkhize's first tooth is wobbling - an enormous thing when you are six. She inspects it in the kettle, the window, the back of a spoon, too scared to touch it and too fascinated to leave it, and she cannot settle to anything. In the bathroom she brushes with Flodent Kids, the paste that starts bright and fades to nothing as she brushes - her own countdown, the rule she knows by heart: brush until the colour is gone, and when it is gone you are done, and when you are done you are brave. She watches the colour clear, and in the quiet after, with no drama, the loose tooth frees in the foam. The brave gap-grin arrives.

But bravery gets a second test. It is nearly time to leave, and a new doubt flickers - will the class think the gap looks silly? One last mirror-check at the door, the clock ticking to school, and she gives herself the grin one more time. It holds. She wears the gap like a medal all day - shows Gogo, shows Mom, shows anyone who will look - the changing colour of the paste and the changing of her smile, the same small magic, both entirely real.

That evening she tucks the tooth under her pillow while the whole family watches from the doorway, proud of the smallest woman in the house.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

Every parent has lived the first wobbly tooth - the terror and the triumph of it. Buhle at kid-scale carries the series ritual with total sincerity, and the colour-change paste turns the brush into a genuine three-second suspense beat: will it clear? The payoff - a tooth in the foam, a gap in the grin - is a real childhood milestone, shot documentary-straight, no invented magic. It is recognisable, and therefore shareable.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

The colour-change is the actual Flodent Kids product doing the actual job - teaching a child to brush long enough - so the brand is the mechanism of the story, not a decoration on it. It plants Flodent Kids as the paste that makes brushing a game a child wants to win, and it seats the youngest Mkhize as a returning face for the kids' line. In Women's Month, the girl-child is the future of the rock.
