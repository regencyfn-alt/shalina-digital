## THE STORY

Pre-dawn, and the day's demands are already stacking against Zimkitha - a lost shoe, a leak under the sink, everyone running late, all of it hers to catch. She brushes in the dark before any of them are up, and she does it properly, because she knows exactly what kind of day this is: there will be no second chance. Not at nine, not at noon, not at four. The bathroom belongs to somebody else from the moment the house wakes, and she will not be back in it until the day is finished with her. Flodent at the mirror, one breath that is nobody's but hers - eish, long day, but I still smile big - and then the clock takes over and she solves it, all of it, and gets them out the door still smiling.

Then her own day begins, and it does not stop, and every single thing she fixes she fixes up close. The teacher who pulls her aside at the gate, heads together over a problem that cannot wait. The neighbour's crisis on the pavement, hand on an arm, face to face. The toddler on her hip who wants to tell her something enormous in a whisper. The job done well and the school run there and back and there again. She is stretched too thin and something of her own is about to slip, and she never asks for help. But the morning is still with her - **Flodent Original's freshness has lasted the whole way into the afternoon**, so not once, in a day made entirely of leaning in, does she lean back from anybody. She does not think about it. That is the point: she has enough to think about.

Evening turns the story over. She walks in braced for the last shift and finds the fixing already done - the family has cooked, the load lifted off her shoulders for once. She laughs so she will not cry, and the family watches her from the doorway - for one evening, looking after the woman who spends every day looking after them.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

Every household has a Zimkitha, and most of them are watched by people who love them and rarely say it. The episode names the invisible labour without ever whining about it - it celebrates her range instead, and the ending, the fixer quietly getting fixed, is the emotional gut-punch of the set. The one-brush truth is the detail that will make women message each other: *this is my whole life, I brush at five and that is it.* It is the Women's Month message delivered as tenderness, not a lecture.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

The problem is real, structural and entirely non-medical: a woman who gets one brush a day and spends that day at conversational distance from everyone she helps. Long-lasting freshness is not a nice-to-have in that life, it is the thing that lets her keep showing up close - and the film proves it by showing five close encounters and never once showing her hesitate. **The benefit is demonstrated, not stated.** It sits at the heart of the Family Values and Start Fresh pillars: resilience, warmth, a family that flourishes because she holds it together - and, this once, holds her back.
