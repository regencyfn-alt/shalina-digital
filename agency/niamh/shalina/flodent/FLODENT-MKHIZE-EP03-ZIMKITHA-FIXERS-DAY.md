## THE STORY

Pre-dawn, and the day's demands are already stacking against Zimkitha - a lost shoe, a leak under the sink, everyone running late, all of it hers to catch. The morning brush is her one still second, Flodent at the mirror, one breath that is nobody's but hers - eish, long day, but I still smile big - and then the clock takes over: school starts in minutes. She solves it, all of it, and gets them out the door still smiling.

Then her own day begins, and it does not stop - the job done well, a neighbour's crisis on the pavement, the school run there and back and there again. She is stretched too thin and something of her own is about to slip, and she never asks for help, racing the last of the day home before it all catches up. But this evening turns the story over: she walks in and the fixing is already done - the family has cooked, the load lifted off her shoulders for once.

She laughs so she will not cry, and the family watches her from the doorway - for one evening, looking after the woman who spends every day looking after them.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

Every household has a Zimkitha, and most of them are watched by people who love them and rarely say it. The episode names the invisible labour without ever whining about it - it celebrates her range instead, and the ending, the fixer quietly getting fixed, is the emotional gut-punch of the set. It is the Women's Month message delivered as tenderness, not a lecture.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

Zimkitha's smile is the thing she gives away all day and never runs out of - the brush is the one moment she does something for herself, and Flodent is that moment. The brand becomes the small, daily act of self-respect that lets a woman keep giving. It sits at the heart of the Family Values and Start Fresh pillars: resilience, warmth, a family that flourishes because she holds it together - and, this once, holds her back.
