## SET INTENT — why these four, this month

August is South Africa's Women's Month. The set puts the Mkhize women at the centre of their own full days — Buhle, Gogo Nomalanga, and Zimkitha each carry an episode of real competence, and the throughline is **mastery and joy, never drudgery.** Sibusiso's episode reclaims the "office dad": for one day the women are out owning their hours and he is home in the domestic trenches, and the honouring is done by laughter — you feel what the women carry because you watch him discover it. Gogo as *imbokodo*, the rock, is the emotional spine of the month; Sibusiso is the release valve. Every episode keeps the engine: the brush is the hinge, the family watches the family from the doorway, home is the return, and the claim lands once, at the end.

**Endline — locked for the set:** *Every day is a fresh start.* (Series standard, applied across all four for consistency. Flag: Buhle's July debut closed on "Every smile tells a story" and a Flodent Kids variant "Every day, a braver smile" exists — one word from you locks kids-vs-series; default here is the series line.)

# 1 · BUHLE — "THE COLOUR COUNTDOWN"
### Youngest · girl · 6–7 · The Inspector · changing colours

## THE STORY
It is the morning Buhle's first tooth is wobbling, and a wobbling tooth is an enormous thing when you are six. She inspects it in the kettle, in the window, in the back of a spoon — too scared to touch it, too fascinated to leave it alone. In the bathroom she brushes with Flodent Kids, the paste that starts bright and slowly fades to nothing as she brushes — her own little countdown, the rule she's learned by heart: brush until the colour's gone, and when it's gone, you're done, and when you're done, you're brave. She watches the colour clear. And in the quiet after, with no drama and no fuss, the tooth that was loose all along comes free in the foam. She stares at the new gap in the mirror and grins the gap-grin — the bravest smile she owns. All day she wears it like a medal: shows Gogo, shows Mom, shows anyone who'll look. The changing colour of the paste and the changing of her smile, the same small magic, both entirely real. Evening, she tucks the tooth under her pillow while the whole family watches from the doorway, proud of the smallest woman in the house.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT
Every parent has lived the first wobbly tooth — the terror and the triumph of it. Buhle at kid-scale carries the series ritual with total sincerity, and the colour-change paste turns the brush into a genuine three-second suspense beat: will it clear? The payoff — a tooth in the foam, a gap in the grin — is a real childhood milestone, shot documentary-straight, no invented magic. It is shareable because it is recognisable.

## WHY THE BRAND BENEFITS
The colour-change is the actual Flodent Kids product doing the actual job — teaching a child to brush long enough — so the brand is the mechanism of the story, not a decoration on it. It plants Flodent Kids as the paste that makes brushing a game a child wants to win, and it seats the youngest Mkhize as a returning face for the kids' line. In Women's Month, the girl-child is the future of the "rock."

# 2 · GOGO NOMALANGA — "IMBOKODO"
### 67 · Gogo No Filter · the rock

## THE STORY
It is Women's Month, and Gogo Nomalanga is running the whole block whether or not the block asked her to. She brushes with Flodent at the mirror — not for courage, she has never been short of that, but because a woman does not go out into her day with anything less than her best smile. Then out she goes, and the day is hers: the market, where she haggles like it's a sport and wins; the clinic queue, which she quietly reorganises so the oldest and the youngest sit first; the young ones on the corner, whom she corrects and feeds in the very same breath. She is the rock the proverb means — *wathint' abafazi, wathint' imbokodo*, you strike a woman, you strike a rock — and she carries a full day of doing on shoulders that have carried far more than this. No filter, all sun: she says the true thing to everyone she meets, and because it comes wrapped in that smile, it always lands warm. By evening the whole street runs a little smoother for her having moved through it. Home on the stoep, she holds court, telling the day back bigger than it was, and the family teases her without mercy and loves her without limit.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT
Everyone knows a Gogo like this — the matriarch who runs more of the neighbourhood than any office ever could. She is funny because she is fearless, and moving because her strength is offered as care. *Imbokodo* is the exact cultural note of Women's Month in South Africa, and putting a 67-year-old woman at the centre of a fast, joyful day is the kind of representation the feed rewards and shares.

## WHY THE BRAND BENEFITS
Gogo's smile is her instrument — the thing that lets her say hard truths softly and get the car moved, the queue fixed, the young one fed. Flodent is what keeps that instrument sharp at 67. The brand sits inside a life of competence and community, exactly the Fresh Start pillar: families with values who flourish and put community first. She is the most quotable face in the cast; her episodes travel.

# 3 · ZIMKITHA — "THE FIXER'S DAY"
### 38 · Mom · The Fixer

## THE STORY
Zimkitha is the one whose day never stops — and this month we follow every hour of it and call it by its real name: work. The morning brush is the only still second she gets, Flodent at the mirror, one breath that belongs to nobody but her — *eish, long day, but I still smile big.* Then the machine of the day starts and she is the engine of it: the household run before anyone's awake enough to thank her, the job done well and on time, the neighbour's problem solved on the pavement, the school run there and back and there again, three people's small catastrophes caught in mid-air before any of them touch the ground. She does not complain and she does not miss a beat; the competence is the point, not the burden — you see the mastery in it, the small private satisfactions, the smile she chooses again and again because choosing it is its own kind of strength. And then, in the evening, something rare: she comes home and the fixing is already done. The family has cooked. The load is lifted off her shoulders for once. She laughs so that she will not cry, and the family watches her from the doorway — for one evening, looking after the woman who spends every day looking after them.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT
Every household has a Zimkitha, and most of them are watched by people who love them and rarely say it. The episode names the invisible labour without ever whining about it — it celebrates her range instead, and the ending (the fixer, quietly, getting fixed) is the emotional gut-punch of the set. It is the Women's Month message delivered as tenderness, not a lecture.

## WHY THE BRAND BENEFITS
Zimkitha's smile is the thing she gives away all day and never runs out of — the brush is the one moment she does something for herself, and Flodent is that moment. The brand becomes the small, daily act of self-respect that lets a woman keep giving. It sits at the heart of the Family Values and Start Fresh pillars: resilience, warmth, a family that flourishes because she holds it together — and, this once, holds her back.

# 4 · SIBUSISO — "MAN OF THE HOUSE"
### 42 · Dad · Mr Positive — the office dad, reclaimed

## THE STORY
The women are out owning their days, so Sibusiso is home to *handle things* — and how hard, he reasons, can that possibly be. The morning brush comes with full bravado: Flodent at the mirror, the confidence speech he actually believes — clear breath, clear head, clear win, *today is mine to take.* And then the day sets about teaching him. The shelf he is "building" fights back and wins on points. The lawnmower will not start, then starts too well, then defeats him in open combat with the flowerbed. The washing outnumbers him. Lunch becomes a negotiation with a small child, and he loses. The office dad, out of his office and gloriously out of his depth, watches every confident line the brush gave him get deflated by a domestic day that the women do without breaking stride. But — and this is the man's whole worth — he sticks it. Sweaty, humbled, foam-flecked and grass-stained, by evening he has *mostly* mowed the lawn and *nearly* levelled the shelf. The women come home to the beautiful wreckage of his effort and to something new in his eyes: real respect for what they carry every single day. The honouring is done by laughter, not a lecture, and the whole family stands in the doorway creasing up.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT
It is the funniest episode of the set and the most quietly feminist — the joke is never on the women, it is on the man who assumed their day was easy. Physical comedy (the mower, the shelf, the toddler negotiation) makes it a natural for the feed, and the turn — bravado to genuine respect — gives it a heart under the laughs. In Women's Month, reclaiming the "provider" dad by showing him humbled at home is a sharp, shareable inversion.

## WHY THE BRAND BENEFITS
Sibusiso's brush is peak "confidence speech" — the exact New-Loud-Alive brand voice — set up so the day can gently puncture it, which makes the ritual land as character, not slogan. Flodent is the fresh start he keeps needing all day as reality resets his ego one chore at a time. He carries all the "chores are hard" comedy so the three women's episodes never have to, protecting the celebration tone of the whole set. Mr Positive stays positive; he just learns what positivity costs.
