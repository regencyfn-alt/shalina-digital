## THE STORY

The women are out owning their day, so Sibusiso is home to handle things - how hard can it be. The morning brush comes with full bravado, Flodent at the mirror, the confidence speech he actually believes: clear breath, clear head, clear win, today is mine to take. Then the day starts teaching him. The shelf he is building fights back, the mower goes to war with the flowerbed, and he is on the clock - the women are back at five. He wrestles one thing into half-working order, and his confidence comes roaring back, entirely unearned.

Emboldened, he takes on lunch and the washing at once - and the day escalates. The toddler negotiation is lost outright, the chaos compounds, and then the worst clock of all: headlights in the drive. They are home, and it is not done. They find the beautiful wreckage of his effort and something new in his eyes - real respect for what they carry every single day.

The honouring is done by laughter, not a lecture, and the whole family stands in the doorway creasing up.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

It is the funniest episode of the set and the most quietly feminist - the joke is never on the women, it is on the man who assumed their day was easy. Physical comedy (the mower, the shelf, the toddler negotiation) makes it a natural for the feed, and the turn - bravado to genuine respect - gives it a heart under the laughs. In Women's Month, reclaiming the provider dad by showing him humbled at home is a sharp, shareable inversion.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

Sibusiso's brush is peak confidence speech - the exact New-Loud-Alive brand voice - set up so the day can gently puncture it, which makes the ritual land as character, not slogan. Flodent is the fresh start he keeps needing all day as reality resets his ego one chore at a time. He carries all the chores-are-hard comedy so the three women's episodes never have to, protecting the celebration tone of the whole set. Mr Positive stays positive; he just learns what positivity costs.
