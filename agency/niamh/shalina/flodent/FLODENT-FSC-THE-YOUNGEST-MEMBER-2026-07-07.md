## THE STORY (30 seconds)

Amahle Mkhize, nine years old, and the worst thing in the world has happened: she and her best friend Lelethu are not speaking. Yesterday, break time, the friendship bracelet Lelethu made her snapped in the fight — she didn't even mean the thing she said — and now the beads are in her pocket in a knot of thread and her stomach hurts the way it only hurts when it's your fault. Tonight she sits at the kitchen table not eating, rolling one bead under her finger. Nobody lectures her. This family doesn't work that way.

But she knows what this family *does* do. She's seen it three times: Papa brushed and gave away his toolbox. Gogo brushed before her big day. Mama brushed and came home with Gudi's scholarship. Big things need a fresh start, and fresh starts start at the sink.

Morning. Amahle drags the plastic step-stool to the bathroom basin — it screeches on the tiles, she winces, freezes, carries it the rest of the way. She takes the Flodent exactly the way the grown-ups do, brushes with enormous nine-year-old seriousness, and gives the mirror the look. The family look. *Today is the day.*

She acts: at the kitchen table, tongue out in concentration, she re-threads the bracelet — and where the broken beads are missing, she threads in three of her own. The good ones. The gold ones she never lets anyone touch.

The school gate. Lelethu, arms folded. Amahle doesn't make a speech — she's a Mkhize. She holds out the bracelet. Lelethu sees the gold beads, knows exactly what they cost, and the arms unfold. Bracelet on wrist; a shoulder-bump; they run for the gate together like yesterday never happened.

From the car window, Zimkhita watches her daughter go — the exact watching the whole family does. The youngest member just joined the club. Packshot. Flodent. Fresh Start.

## WHY KIDS WILL RELATE (the brief's ask, straight)
- A best-friend fight feels world-ending at nine, and *being the one who was wrong* is the loneliest feeling in school.
- Saying sorry first is the bravest thing a kid ever does — braver than anything the adults did in their episodes, and the film quietly knows it.
- Kids copy adult rituals when nobody's watching — the step-stool at the basin is every child who ever wanted to be big.
- The gold beads are a real price. Kids know exactly what it means to give away the thing you never let anyone touch.
- No adult fixes anything. Nobody lectures. She does it herself — kid agency, start to finish.
