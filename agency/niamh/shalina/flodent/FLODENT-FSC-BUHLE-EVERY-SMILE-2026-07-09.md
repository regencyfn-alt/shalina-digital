## THE STORY (30 seconds · Shane's cuts, timed)

It's Show and Tell morning, and Buhle Mkhize has been ready for days. At the kitchen table she finishes colouring the picture that's going to school with her: her whole family, and she's given every one of them a big bright smile — that's the point of the picture, that's her family. She sets it down carefully as Zimkhita calls her for breakfast.

Then the world ends the way it ends when you're eight: reaching for her cup, she tips it — tea across the table, across the drawing. The colours run. A big brown stain right through her family. Buhle freezes. Devastated. Nobody rushes in with a speech — this family doesn't work that way.

Bathroom, before school. She brushes her teeth with Flodent the way she's watched the others do it, and gives the mirror the look — and the smile that comes back at her is the thing the drawing was always about. Confidence walks back in. And with it, the noticing: held in her hands, the stain isn't ruin. The stain is shaped like a *tree*.

Back at the table, tongue out in concentration: the stain becomes a great spreading family tree, and she redraws her family beneath its branches, every face wearing the bright smile — and on a shelf in the corner of the drawing, tiny and exact, the Flodent tube, because in this house that's where mornings start.

School. Buhle presents her artwork and tells them the truth: the mistake became her favourite part of the picture — because every morning starts with a smile. Her classmates applaud. The teacher hangs it on the wall.

Closing: Buhle runs home to tell everyone — the drawing isn't just a picture anymore, it's a story. Packshot.

**End frame: Flodent. Every smile tells a story.**
