## What This Is

This document governs every script written under the Flodent Fresh Start Club series. It defines the format, the character architecture, the product rules, and the structural template. Any writer working on this series reads this first. No exceptions.

## 1. The Series Concept

**Fresh Start Club** is a family-based series set across two African markets. Each market has one permanent family. Each episode follows a different family member through a moment that matters — the day someone decides to change something. Flodent sits at that turning point.

The tone is warm, grounded, and earned. Not aspirational-glossy. Real families, real communities, real moments — with one clean product truth: Flodent is what you reach for before you step into the moment that matters.

**The series question** is always the same: *What does it look like when someone in this family decides to change something?* The family is the spine. Individual members carry episodes. The audience follows the household, not just one face.

**The two families:**
- **Joburg (South Africa) — The Mkhize family.** Dad, mum (Zimkhita), and their extended household. Four moments per character per year. SA runs flagship — TikTok-led, high cadence.
- **Angola — A separate family.** Different cast, same format. Angola runs relationship-led — Meta-anchored, quieter cadence. The brand voice flexes by market; the structure doesn't.

**Six characters total** across both markets and both families. The audience adopts the cast — Mkhize dad, Mkhize mum (Zimkhita), and their family in SA; the Angolan family in their market. Always-on beats episodic. The cast becomes a household, not an ad.

## 2. The Product Rule (Non-Negotiable)

> **Flodent must be the hinge, not the decoration.**

The product moment is the scene where the lead character moves from hesitation to action, from preparation to readiness, from "not yet" to "now." Brushing with Flodent is the physical act that marks that transition.

**What the product moment IS:**
- The lead character reaching for Flodent before the moment that matters
- A clean product shot: tube visible, label readable, in use
- Immediately followed by the action the character has been building toward

**What the product moment is NOT:**
- Background set dressing in a bathroom scene
- A toothbrushing cameo with no story function
- A "bright idea" moment that could have been triggered by anything

**Test:** Remove the Flodent scene. Does the story still make sense? If yes, it's decoration. If the product is the hinge, removing it breaks the arc. Every script must pass this test.

## 3. The Four-Beat Engine

This is the master framework. Every Fresh Start Club story — every market, every family member, every episode — runs on this engine. No exceptions.

| Beat | Name | The Job |
|------|------|---------|
| **01** | **RELATE** | We feel the character before we know their name. One image, one truth. We are them. |
| **02** | **CONFLICT** | Something is in the way. Not a catastrophe — a real, human weight. The gap between where they are and where they need to be. |
| **03** | **TIME** | The frame narrows. This moment, this morning, this decision. The clock is emotional, not literal. Flodent sits here — the thing they reach for before they step into it. |
| **04** | **RESOLUTION** | The brand value lands. Not as a product. As a feeling. They moved. Something changed. Fresh start delivered. |

> **This repeats across every video in the series.** Same engine. Different family member. Different market. Different weight. The audience learns the rhythm and leans in earlier each time — because they know what's coming and they want it.

The power is in the repetition. Each episode conditions the audience. By episode three, they feel the resolution before it happens. That's not formula. That's *trust*.

## 3a. Story Structure — The 6-Beat Execution

Every Fresh Start Club story runs **30–40 seconds maximum** across 6 beats. Linear. No loops, no subplots, no extended sequences. One character drives it start to finish.

| Beat | Name | Function | Approx Time |
|------|------|----------|-------------|
| 01 | **The Problem** | Lead character sees something they want to fix — felt, not explained | 0–6s |
| 02 | **The Fresh Start Brush** | PRODUCT MOMENT — freshness triggers the decision | 6–12s |
| 03 | **She Acts** | She moves — baking, travelling, arriving. Energy forward. | 12–20s |
| 04 | **The Turn** | Something shifts in her favour — the win, the moment | 20–28s |
| 05 | **The Fresh Start** | She delivers. The person receives it. Simple. | 28–35s |
| 06 | **Packshot + Endline** | Flodent. Fresh Start. | 35–40s |

> **Beat 02 is locked.** Product moment is always second — the freshness is what sparks the plan. Non-negotiable.

> **30–40 seconds is the ceiling.** One location per beat. One character driving. If a scene doesn't move the story forward, it doesn't exist.

## 4. Character Architecture — The Answer to Misbah

This is the direct response to the concern: *"How many new characters will we keep introducing? When do they start repeating?"*

**The answer is structural, not a promise.**

### The Two Families (Permanent)

Each market has ONE permanent family. The audience follows the family across the series. Individual family members carry individual episodes, but the household stays constant.

**South Africa — The Mkhize Family (Joburg)**

| Character | Role | Archetype |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| **Zimkhita** | The mother | The Fixer — she acts on feeling, not plans |
| **Mkhize dad** | The father | TBC per episode brief |
| Family / extended household | Supporting | Gudi (son, musician), others as needed per episode |

**Angola — The Angolan Family** *(cast TBC, to be named in Angola production brief)*

| Character | Role | Archetype |
|-----------|------|-----------|

### The Rule in Plain Language

> The faces you see across the series are the family's. The moment you follow is one family member's. The audience accumulates knowledge of the household — through individual stories, not a rotating cast.

No new families are introduced within a market. New faces enter as one-episode community characters only. The family is permanent.

### Supporting Characters (One Episode)

People who exist within an episode's story — neighbours, stokvel members, the child who receives the fresh start — do not recur unless a story specifically calls for it. They are texture, not cast.

- **Gudi** (Zimkhita's son) — recurs as a presence in the Mkhize family's life; doesn't need to carry another episode, but can appear in the background
- **The stokvel** — recurring setting and community, not a named recurring cast
- One-episode story characters don't graduate to permanent cast

## 6. The Tone Rules

1. **Earned warmth.** Not saccharine. The emotion lands because the story earns it — through a real obstacle, a real decision, a real reveal.
2. **Comedy is allowed — briefly.** The Granny scene in Zimkhita's story, the bathroom moment in Carlos's story. One beat of lightness per episode, never the centre.
3. **No dialogue that explains the product.** The character doesn't say "Flodent gives me confidence." They brush. They step forward. The audience connects it.
4. **Community, not celebrity.** These are ordinary people doing extraordinary things quietly. No aspirational gloss. Authentic environments, authentic wardrobes, authentic faces.
5. **The lead doesn't need credit.** Zimkhita doesn't get applause for the scholarship. She watches Gudi's face. That's the scene.

## 7. PPB Guardrails (All Markets)

- No healthcare professionals shown or quoted
- No "cure" language
- No before/after comparisons
- Product clearly visible in Beat 02 (tube, label, in-use)
- Brand claim appears only at the packshot (Beat 08)
- VO lines remain aspirational, not clinical

## 9. Character Continuity — The Series Arc

This section answers Misbah's question directly: *how do the characters develop, and how do their storylines connect?*

### The Family's Ongoing Life

The Mkhize family is not reset between episodes. Their world carries forward. What happened in Episode 01 (Zimkhita wins the stokvel pot, Gudi gets his scholarship) is part of who they are in Episode 02. The audience accumulates knowledge of the household — not through recap, but through small details that reward people watching the whole series.

**The Mkhize family arc:**

| Episode | Who carries it | What they do | What we learn |
|---------|---------------|--------------|---------------|
| 01 — The Mother's Fresh Start | Zimkhita (mum) | Wins stokvel pot, pays Gudi's scholarship | She acts on feeling, not plans |
| 02 — The Father's Day | Mkhize dad | TBC — the day he's been dreading | He handles pressure quietly |
| 03+ | TBC family member | TBC | The pattern deepens: this family gives before it receives |
| Future | Any member | At some point, the family receives | The series turn: the givers get given to |

> **The series turn** is the long game. The audience has been watching this family give fresh starts to everyone around them. When it's their turn to receive one — collectively or individually — it lands because the audience knows them. That is the continuity payoff.

### Cross-Market

The Joburg family and the Angolan family are in separate markets. They do not interact. The series connection between markets is the brand and the format — not character crossovers. Each family has their own world.

### What to Tell Misbah

The continuity she's looking for is **the Mkhize family's evolving life**, not a large recurring ensemble. The series is built to grow around one household, deepen them over time, and eventually give them the same moment they've been giving everyone else. That's the arc. Six characters total across two markets — the audience always has someone to follow.

## 10. The Writer Brief (One Page)

When briefing a scriptwriter on a new episode, give them:

1. **This document** (the series bible)
2. **The lead character's dossier** (who Zimkhita is, what she's already done in prior episodes)
3. **One sentence:** What fresh start does she give this episode, and to whom?
4. **One constraint:** What is the Flodent hinge moment — what is she about to do when she brushes?

Everything else is the writer's job. The structure holds them. The hinge keeps the product honest.
