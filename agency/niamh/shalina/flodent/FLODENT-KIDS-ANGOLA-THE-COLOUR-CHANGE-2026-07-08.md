## THE STORY (30 seconds)

Zeca is the fastest seven-year-old in the bairro, and it is ruining his life. He buttons his shirt in four seconds — wrong holes. He eats his breakfast funge like the plate owes him money. And tomorrow is the festa da escola, where he must stand in front of everyone and recite the poem — the one he rushes so badly that at practice in the yard the words fall over each other and his cousins laugh. Avó Domingas watches from her chair and says nothing. This family doesn't lecture. But that evening, unpacking the shopping, Mamã sets one new thing on the shelf above the basin: a small blueberry tube. "It changes colour," she says, to nobody in particular. "But only if you give it time."

Morning of the festa. Zeca climbs to the basin, squeezes the blue paste, and brushes his usual three furious seconds — nothing. He stops. Looks. Brushes again, slower, watching the mirror, and the colour begins to turn — and Zeca, for the first time in his seven years, waits for something. The change completes. He grins at the mirror with his whole face. *Today is the day.*

The festa. His name is called. He walks up too fast — then stops, feet together, and takes the breath. The same wait. And he gives the poem slowly, word by word, the way it was written to be given. The courtyard goes quiet in the good way. Avó Domingas's face does what grandmothers' faces do. From the back, Mamã films twenty shaky seconds and sends it — we hear the WhatsApp whoosh — to everyone.

The paste taught him. Nobody else had to. Packshot. Flodent Kids. Fresh Start. ⟨PT-HELD: endline + sign-off for Elvis⟩

## HINGE TEST (bible §2)

Remove Cut 2 and the arc breaks: Cut 1 is a boy who cannot slow down; Cut 3 is a boy who stops and breathes. The colour change is where he learns the wait — the product doesn't accompany the lesson, it IS the lesson. Nothing else in the story could teach it. Pass — and the kids variant passes it in a way no adult episode can, because here the product feature and the story engine are the same mechanism.

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND ×10

1. The colour change is the product's real, ownable feature — the story is built on it, not near it.
2. Beat 02 locked: the product moment is second and sparks everything after.
3. The paste is the teacher — no parent explains the product, honouring the no-explaining-dialogue rule.
4. Colour-change-as-timer reframes the 2-minute brush from chore to game — the behaviour the brand actually needs kids to adopt.
5. Blueberry tube and label carry the shot in close-up at 9:16 — shelf recognition, which the Angola treatment names as critical for cantina retail.
6. Angola register honoured: three generations, food, patience, warmth — no SA urban sharpness imported.
7. WhatsApp send is diegetic — the campaign's hub channel appears inside the story, not bolted on.
8. PPB-clean: no professionals, no cure language, no comparisons; claim at packshot only.
9. Series engine intact — relate, conflict, time, resolution — so the episode slots into the club without depending on any prior story.
10. Launch logic: a variant launch told as a story costs the audience nothing and gives retail a face for the new tube.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE ×10

1. Every Angolan household has a Zeca — the kid who does everything at double speed.
2. The wrong buttons and the demolished funge are laugh-of-recognition images, not jokes at the child's expense.
3. Being laughed at by your cousins is a real seven-year-old wound — small to adults, enormous to him.
4. The grandmother who watches and says nothing is deeply familiar — authority as presence, not speeches.
5. Kids watching want to see the colour change with their own eyes — the film is a product demo disguised as a dare.
6. The first wait of his life is visible on his face, not narrated — kids read faces faster than words.
7. Reciting at the school festa is a universal Angolan childhood rite — every parent watching has filmed one.
8. Mamã's shaky twenty seconds and the WhatsApp whoosh is exactly how Angolan families witness things — the audience does this weekly.
9. Kid agency start to finish: no adult fixes anything; he solves himself at the basin.
10. The lesson travels beyond the tube — parents share the film because it argues for patience, and the brand rides the share.
