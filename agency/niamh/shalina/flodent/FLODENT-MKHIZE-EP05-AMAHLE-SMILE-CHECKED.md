## THE STORY

It is the day Amahle leads for the first time - front and centre of the showcase, the younger ones on her count instead of the other way round - and the nerves are the teenage kind that live in the mirror. Bathroom, before the hall: she brushes her teeth with Flodent the way the whole family does, tube in shot, in use, then looks up. It is the ritual she has had since she was small, the one Gogo does, that Mom does - the big smile, the line she tells herself, the clock ticking to curtain: *Smile checked. Voice checked. Let's go.* She steadies, a beat ahead of her own fear, and walks in ready.

On stage, front and centre, it almost comes apart - a wobble mid-routine, the line nearly breaking, real stakes in real time, seconds to save it. And she does: she carries them through, not flawless, fully herself, joyful and in command, and the little ones follow her the way she once followed Gogo.

Home that evening, she is teaching Buhle the first eight-count in the kitchen - four generations of women's confidence handed down the line - while the family watches from the doorway, proud.
