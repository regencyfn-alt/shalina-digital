## THE STORY

The women are out owning their day, so Sibusiso is home to handle things - how hard can it be. The morning brush comes with full bravado, tube in shot at the family mirror, the confidence speech he actually believes: clear head, clear win, today is mine to take. Then the day starts teaching him. The shelf he is building fights back. The mower goes to war with the flowerbed and the flowerbed does not survive the negotiation. And he is on the clock the whole time, because the women are back at five and this house is supposed to be standing. He wrestles one thing into half-working order, steps back to admire it, and his confidence comes roaring back entirely unearned.

Emboldened, he takes on lunch and the washing at once, which is where the day escalates properly. The toddler negotiation is lost outright and lost badly. The chaos compounds in the way chaos does when a confident man is running it. And then the worst clock of all: headlights in the drive. They are home, and it is not done, and there is no version of the next ninety seconds that ends with him looking competent. They come in to find the beautiful wreckage of his effort - and something new in his eyes, which is real respect for what these women carry every single day without announcing it.

The honouring is done by laughter, not a lecture, and the whole family stands in the doorway creasing up while he surveys the ruins with as much dignity as he can still locate. Later, quietly, the family tube goes back on the bathroom shelf where it lives, next to everybody else's things, in a house that is running again because they are all in it.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

It is the funniest episode of the set and the most quietly feminist - the joke is never on the women, it is on the man who assumed their day was easy. The physical comedy of the mower, the shelf and the toddler negotiation makes it a natural for the feed, and the turn from bravado to genuine respect gives it a heart under the laughs. In Women's Month, reclaiming the provider dad by showing him humbled at home is a sharp, shareable inversion that men will send to each other and women will send with a single word attached.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

Sibusiso's brush is peak confidence speech - the exact New-Loud-Alive brand voice - set up deliberately so the day can puncture it, which makes the ritual land as character rather than slogan. Flodent is the household constant underneath a day of chaos: the one shelf everybody shares, still there when everything else has fallen over. He carries all the chores-are-hard comedy so the three women's episodes never have to, protecting the celebration tone of the whole set. Mr Positive stays positive; he just learns what positivity costs.
