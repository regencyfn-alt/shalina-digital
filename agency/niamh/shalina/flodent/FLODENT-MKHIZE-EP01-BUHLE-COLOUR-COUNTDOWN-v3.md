## THE STORY

It is show-and-tell morning, and Buhle Mkhize's first tooth is wobbling - an enormous thing when you are six. She inspects it in the kettle, the window, the back of a spoon, too scared to touch it and too fascinated to leave it, and she cannot settle to anything for more than a moment. The brushing goes the way it always goes when a six-year-old has something better to think about: three furious seconds and she is off the step and away, and Mom sends her back. She is not being naughty. She simply has no idea what *done* looks like, and nothing in the bathroom has ever told her.

Then the paste takes the decision away from her. Flodent Kids starts bright and fades as she brushes - brush until the colour is gone, and when it is gone you are done - and it cannot be rushed and it cannot be cheated, because the colour is either there or it is not. So for the first time all morning Buhle stands still. She watches the mirror instead of the clock, working her way around on the paste's timing rather than her own, and the bright fades slowly out of the foam. In the quiet after - no drama, no pulling, no string on a door handle - the loose tooth frees itself in the froth. The brave gap-grin arrives and it is enormous.

But bravery gets a second test. It is nearly time to leave and a new doubt flickers: will the class think the gap looks silly? One last mirror-check at the door, the clock ticking to school, and she gives herself the grin one more time. It holds - she has already done the hard thing once today, and waiting is something she now knows how to do. She wears the gap like a medal all day, shows Gogo, shows Mom, shows anyone who will look. That evening she tucks the tooth under her pillow while the whole family watches from the doorway, proud of the smallest woman in the house.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

Every parent has lived the first wobbly tooth - the terror and the triumph of it - and every parent has also lived the three-second brush and the child who will not stand still. Buhle carries the series ritual at kid-scale with total sincerity, and the colour-change turns the brush into a genuine suspense beat: will it clear before she gives up? The payoff - a tooth in the foam, a gap in the grin - is a real childhood milestone shot documentary-straight, with no invented magic. It is recognisable, and therefore shareable.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

This is the clearest product-as-solver episode in the set, and it needs no claim to get there. The problem is real and universal - children do not brush long enough because they cannot tell when to stop - and Flodent Kids is the only thing in the story that solves it. The colour countdown is the actual product doing the actual job, so the brand is the mechanism of the story rather than a decoration on it. It plants Flodent Kids as the paste that makes brushing a game a child wants to win, and it seats the youngest Mkhize as a returning face for the kids' line. In Women's Month, the girl-child is the future of the rock.
