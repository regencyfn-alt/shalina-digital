> **Note for the client, said openly.** The brief asked for the product to visibly drive the outcome. Gogo's problem is never her mouth - she is fearless, and that is the entire woman. Any version where toothpaste fixes a matriarch's courage makes the brand look ridiculous, and the audience will feel it before they can name it. So on this one Flodent does not solve her; **Flodent is the thing she carries and gives away.** Product as an artifact of her care. Three placements, no dialogue, no claim.

## THE STORY

It is Women's Month, and Gogo Nomalanga is up before the block that needs her. By the door is the shelf of small things she keeps stocked for other people - the plasters, the paracetamol, the tube of Flodent - and she takes what she thinks the street will need today and puts it in her bag, the way she takes everything for everyone. Then she is out, straight into the first fire: the clinic queue is chaos and an elder is about to be turned away before the doors close. The clock is against her. Gogo reorders the whole queue with a smile and one true word nobody else in that room would say, and the elder is seen. Behind her at the corner, the Flodent billboard she has walked past every day of her life.

Word travels fast, and by midday the street brings her everything - including the thing nobody wants to touch: a young one in real trouble, a dispute about to boil over. She has until the day turns to cool it. No filter, all sun, she lands the truth wrapped in that smile, and the block moves because she moved through it. She is the rock the proverb means - wathint' imbokodo, you strike a woman, you strike a rock. On the way home she stops at a door, and leaves one of the small things from her bag with a family who needed it and would never have asked. Nobody says a word about it. She just does it, the way she does everything.

Evening on the stoep, she holds court, telling the day back bigger than it was, and the family teases her without mercy and loves her without limit. On the shelf by the door, the gap where the tube was.

## WHY THE AUDIENCE WILL LOVE IT

Everyone knows a Gogo like this - the matriarch who runs more of the neighbourhood than any office ever could. She is funny because she is fearless, and moving because her strength is offered as care. Imbokodo is the exact cultural note of Women's Month in South Africa, and putting a 67-year-old woman at the centre of a fast, joyful day is the kind of representation the feed rewards and shares. The shelf detail lands quietly and stays with people: she does not just fix problems, she stocks up for other people's.

## WHY THE BRAND WILL LOVE IT

Flodent appears three times and never once interrupts the story: on her shelf, on her corner, and in a stranger's hand. That is a stronger brand position than a product-solves-problem beat could ever be here, because it says something true - **Flodent is what the woman everyone trusts keeps in the house for everyone else.** Being chosen by Gogo Nomalanga is worth more than being credited with her courage, and it is the one claim in this episode that nobody could dispute. It sits squarely in the Family Values and Start Fresh pillars: a household of competence, community first, and the brand woven into the fabric of how a block looks after itself.
