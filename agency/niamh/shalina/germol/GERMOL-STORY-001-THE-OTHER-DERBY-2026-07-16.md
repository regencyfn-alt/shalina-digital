## BRAND SUMMARY

Germol is the antiseptic a household reaches for when the game leaves its mark — the liquid you tip into water and it cleans a scrape, protects the skin, and hands a kid back to his afternoon. It lives by one law: the product is in the image, used, never posed — no bottle to camera, no line spoken over it, no lecture. The cloudy swirl of ASL in water is the whole demonstration; South Africans know that swirl means *clean and safe.* On derby day, rugby is a contact game, and contact is exactly Germol's territory — not the couch the beer brands own, but the grazed knee that would end a smaller brand's afternoon and doesn't end this one. The claim lands once, at the very end: *Shields with Love and Care.*

## CAMPAIGN SUMMARY

"Wash & Brush" runs through the SA winter, and this hero pins it to the August rugby derby — the one weekend the whole country is watching the same match. Germol does not fight beer for the living room. It takes the yard: the kids playing their own derby while the grown-ups watch the real one, and the antiseptic care that keeps those kids in their game. It carries the client brief's R3 (a grass-day scrape treated with ASL, then straight back into play — no blood shown) and R1 (personal hygiene), and it lands the women's-month spine plainly: the person who cleans the knee is the household's wellness lead, and she does it in five seconds without a speech. After watching, a parent recognises their own child, their own scraped-knee Saturday, and the bottle they keep for exactly this.

## STORY SUMMARY (30s)

Derby day. Inside, the big match builds — the low roar of a stadium through a doorway, grown-ups leaning toward a screen. Outside, on a strip of winter grass, the **other derby** is on: kids in mismatched jerseys playing their hearts out, a match as serious to them as the one on TV. One boy goes for everything — a dive, a stretch, a try that matters to no one but everyone here. He gets it. And he comes up with a grazed knee, grass and grit down one leg, the sting arriving a beat after the glory.

The afternoon could stop here. It doesn't. The wellness anchor is already moving — no fuss, no drama. She tips Germol Antiseptic Liquid into a basin of water and stirs; the familiar cloudy swirl forms. A cloth, a clean wipe of the knee, the grit gone, the skin cared for — a few seconds of steady hands while, from inside, the pre-kickoff whistle climbs. Care given, not performed.

The boy is up before she's finished packing it away. He looks at the knee — clean, sorted — and looks back at the grass, then at the doorway where the real derby is about to start. He gets both: one more play outside, or a dive onto the couch with the grown-ups for kickoff — either way he's *back in the game*, and he hasn't missed the whistle. The bottle sits quietly by the basin, no fuss made about it. On the settled, ready moment the line lands, once: *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Puts Germol on its **own** territory — antiseptic care for the knocks of a contact sport — instead of the couch-and-cooler moment beer brands already own.
2. Lands the client's R3 reel as a story, not a demo: grass-day scrape → ASL → back in the game, cleanly, with no blood or abrasion shown.
3. The cloudy swirl is the demonstration and the hero image at once — ownable, instantly-read, needs no VO and no label-to-camera.
4. Honours the brand law: product in the image, used, never posed; claim held to a single end frame.
5. Lands the women's-month / decision-maker spine without preaching — the anchor simply acts, competent and quick.
6. The parallel — big derby inside, kids' derby outside — is a clean, ownable structure that ties Germol to the August fixture without touching a single protected mark.
7. Carries winter-season truth (outdoor play, scrapes, skin care) exactly as the brief asks, and reads as care, not fear.
8. Gives the channel plan tight cutdowns: the 10s "swirl → back in" moment, the grass-dive still, and a WhatsApp beat built for derby weekend.
9. Fits the pack-size ask — the ASL bottle sits honestly by the basin in-scene; a soap bar at the sink covers the range shot with no posing.
10. Commercial truth: it makes Germol the reason the afternoon continues — care that keeps a kid in his game — a warmer, stickier role than "kills germs," and a reason to keep it stocked.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. Every SA parent knows the scraped-knee Saturday — they see their own child in three seconds.
2. The scroll stops on the dive: a kid giving everything for a try that means nothing and everything.
3. The two derbies running at once is instantly legible and quietly funny — the little one takes his just as seriously.
4. The sting-after-the-glory beat is true to the body; everyone remembers the graze arriving a second late.
5. The fix is satisfying because it is calm — no panic, no fuss, just someone who has it handled in five seconds.
6. The swirl is oddly lovely to watch and reads as *clean* instantly — it rewards the eye.
7. They trust it because nobody performs the product at them — the bottle sits there like it sits in their own home.
8. It flatters the audience: care is competence here, and the story assumes they already know how to use the stuff.
9. They send it to the parent whose kid is always covered in grass — "this is you this weekend."
10. A week later they remember one image — the cloudy swirl cleaning the knee — and one line, *Shields with Love and Care.*

### FLAGS ON THIS STORY (named, not buried)

1. **Germol image work is GATED — no plates this movement.** The three open Germol rulings — **face/no-face**, **animation-dial**, **livery** — gate ALL Germol image generation. This first movement is prose only; the hero and every frame stay reserved until those rulings land. Nothing generated.

3. **Rugby references must stay regulatory-safe.** Kids in mismatched/generic jerseys, a stadium *sound* through a doorway, a screen never shown in detail — all fine. **No** union/team crest or protected mark, **no** broadcaster or competition logo, **no** official-sponsor implication; any anthem/commentary/crowd audio is post-production only, never baked into generation. 4. **No blood, no abrasion — client rule held.** Per the brief's R3 note, show a grass-and-grit graze and a wince only; the wipe reveals clean skin. Nothing gory, nothing that reads as injury beyond a scuff.
5. **Cast unnamed for Germol SA.** No named ensemble yet (unlike Flodent's Mkhize family). Working roles only. A recurring named Germol SA household is a series decision to take before hero plates.
6. **Length locked at 30s.** Structure is a parallel-cut (inside/outside) resolving on the care beat and kickoff — it holds 30s cleanly; a 20s cut drops the "one more play vs. couch" choice and ends on the clean knee.
7. **Prior draft retained, not deleted.** "MATCH DAY" (braai) stays on disk as the superseded idea — quarantine-not-delete. This derby version is the recommended direction.
