## BRAND SUMMARY (all four films)

Germol ASL is the antiseptic a household trusts with its people and its surfaces — hygiene as an act of love, not fear. Sign-off, locked, Sentence Case, Rubik Bold: **"Shields with Love and Care"** (PT: *"Protegido com amor e carinho"*). Colours: Germol Blue #0091D0 + Safety Green #0C9748 on white, 40/40/20. The buyer is a woman 20–45 who drives her household's health decisions; the brief is winter — colds, dry skin, indoor days, school holidays, football month, SA in the Top 32s. Show pack sizes where the frame allows. No fear language, no germ close-ups played for horror, no blood — ever.

## CAMPAIGN SUMMARY (all four films)

The engine that sold this campaign: **the world as a child sees it.** Adults perform the Germol ritual with quiet confidence; the child watching sees the magic that is actually there — glowing water, shield imprints, armour of light. Shot 1 is reality. Shot 2 is the child's eyes. Shot 3 returns to reality carrying the feeling. The four films answer the client's four required usages in order: personal hygiene · surface disinfecting · the footballer's return · the baby-clothes wash. Together they run a month: the body, the home, the pitch, the family — one protective world, seen truly by the only people who still look properly.

# FILM 1 — THE ARMOUR BATH
### Personal hygiene · Germol ASL in the wash water

## STORY SUMMARY

It is early on a school-holiday morning, and the bathroom is the warmest room in a cold house. A mother runs a shallow bath for her son, who is maybe six, sleep still in his face, wrapped in a towel like a small chief. She holds the Germol ASL bottle where he can see it — because he always wants to see it — and pours one capful into the water. The amber drop falls, blooms, and vanishes.

That is where his eyes take over. In his version of the bathroom, the bloom doesn't fade — it spreads, a slow spiral of Germol Blue turning to Safety Green at its edges, until the whole bath is lit from beneath like a forge. This is not bathwater. This is where armour is made. As his mother washes him — arms, neck, the back of the ears, the ordinary tender choreography — the glowing water runs off his skin and leaves something behind that only he can see: a thin shine, a fitted suit of light. Germ-creatures at the frosted window peer in, assess the situation, and leave without argument. He sits perfectly still, the way one sits for something ceremonial. His mother thinks he is being unusually good. He is being knighted.

The fantasy releases as she lifts him out. Reality: steam, towel, cold floor tiles, socks. But at the front door, dressed for the holiday cold, he pauses — checks his own hands, once, the way his mother checks the stove — and walks out into the winter world without a flinch of doubt. She watches from the doorway, arms folded, unaware she has just armoured a knight for the day. Product hero: ASL bottle at the bath's edge, both pack sizes beside it. *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND
1. Answers the client's Reel 1 requirement — personal-hygiene usage of ASL — with the capful-in-the-bath ritual shown accurately and centrally.
2. The armour metaphor makes the invisible product truth (residual protection on skin) visible without a single scientific claim to defend.
3. Winter brief honoured — cold house, warm bath, dressing for the cold — the seasonal context is the setting, not a caption.
4. The mother is the decision-maker on screen, doing the deciding — women's-month positioning embodied, not stated.
5. Pack sizes appear naturally at the bath edge per client ask.
6. No fear: germ-creatures leave "without argument" — protection with a smile, never threat.
7. Skin-safe balance is implicit in the tenderness — the same water that protects is the water that washes a child gently.
8. Extends the sold engine to a new usage without repeating a sold story — same world, new room.
9. The ritual-visibility (child insists on watching the pour) plants brand behaviour in the next generation of buyers.
10. Sign-off lands on a moment of earned confidence — the brand promise proven inside the story before the line appears.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE
1. Every parent has bathed a half-asleep child in winter — recognition is instant and physical.
2. The capful pour is a real, known gesture — audiences see their own hands doing it.
3. The scroll-stopper is the forge-light bath at second 11 — colour bomb in a muted winter palette.
4. The child sitting ceremonially still while being washed is comedy every parent recognises inverted — usually they will not sit still.
5. "He is being knighted" energy gives the child dignity, not cuteness — audiences share dignity.
6. The doorway pause — a small boy checking his hands like an adult — is the detail people rewatch.
7. Mothers see their unwitnessed morning labour honoured as the making of armour. That is the share.
8. No lecture, no doctor, no chart — trust arrives through story, so guards stay down.
9. Warmth in cold: winter content that feels like the warm room, not the weather report.
10. A week later what remains is the image of glowing bathwater — and the capful that made it.

# FILM 2 — THE DAILY SHIELD
### Surface cleaning & disinfecting · updated from the sold treatment

## STORY SUMMARY

Early, dark-early, the house not yet awake. A working mother in a doek and house coat moves through her kitchen with the efficiency of a woman with forty tasks before seven o'clock. She dilutes Germol ASL into her spray bottle from the cap — measured, unthinking, muscle memory. At the doorway, low, at exactly the height of a five-year-old, a pair of eyes appears. Pyjamas. School-holiday bed-hair. The child does not interrupt. The child is witnessing.

She wipes the counter. In the child's eyes, the surface keeps a shield: a blue imprint of light, Germol Blue, radiating out from her cloth and settling into permanence. The door handle — shield. The tap — shield. The light switch — shield. She moves through the kitchen and the room assembles into a map of protection behind her, a grid of soft blue that she cannot see and will never know about. She does not glow; she is not a superhero, because she is something better — the person who makes the house safe before anyone else is awake to need it. The child turns slowly in the centre of the fortress she is building, seeing all of it.

She straightens, turns, and finds the child in the doorway. The smile she gives is not a performance — it is the quiet one that means *I see you, I'm glad you're here.* The child pads in, places one small hand flat on the door handle she just wiped, and holds it there, feeling for the shield. Then looks up. Something passes between them that needs no line of dialogue: the house is ready now. They made it ready — even if only one of them knows both their parts. Product hero: ASL bottle and spray, second pack size on the shelf behind. *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND
1. Squarely delivers the client's Reel 2 — surface cleaning and disinfecting — as the entire narrative spine.
2. The dilution-from-the-cap detail teaches correct usage inside the story, no tutorial required.
3. The shield-grid is the brand's own asset (Shield, per R3 guideline) made narrative — brand device, not borrowed effect.
4. Ms. Germol is present in spirit: any real woman doing this routine becomes her — the character scales to UGC without casting.
5. Women as household-health decision-makers is the film's whole subject, per client trends note.
6. Winter-indoor context — surfaces matter most when everyone is home; the brief's anxiety answered without fear language.
7. Blue #0091D0 owns the fantasy layer — colour proportion delivered inside the story world.
8. Pack sizes placed on the shelf per client ask, incidental and real.
9. This was the standard-setter treatment — retained and sharpened, anchoring the four-film set in the world that sold.
10. The sign-off lands on maternal affirmation, fusing brand promise with the buyer's self-image.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE
1. The 25–45 mother sees her own 6am self on screen within three seconds — doek, house coat, spray bottle.
2. Being watched by a child from a doorway is a universally lived moment — from both sides.
3. Scroll-stop: the first shield imprint blooming under the cloth at second 12.
4. The grid building room-wide rewards continued watching — the pay-off compounds.
5. Affirmation, not aspiration: her unwitnessed work is seen and named as protection. That recognition is rare and it lands.
6. The child testing the handle for the shield is the rewatch detail.

10. What remains a week later: every door handle in their own house, briefly imagined blue.

# FILM 3 — THE WINNING RETURN
### The footballer · client's scripted beat, no blood, branded jersey

## STORY SUMMARY

A township pitch in late-winter gold, the hour when every shadow is long and every game matters. A teenage player chases a fifty-fifty ball, mistimes the slide, and goes down — a grass cut on the knee, nothing more. No blood, no drama; just the sting every player knows and the game pausing around him. At the fence, small fingers grip the wire: a child, six years old, his most loyal supporter, watching what happens next with total attention.

The coach jogs on with the kit bag. Out comes the Germol ASL bottle and cotton wool — the sideline ritual every South African football family knows by heart. Dab, clean, done, quick and practiced. And as the ASL touches the graze, the child at the fence sees what is really happening: light spreads from the knee — Germol Blue running up the leg, across the shoulders, over the whole player — an armouring. The dark little germ-creatures that had gathered hopefully around the cut scatter like a beaten defence. The player stands inside a suit of light only one spectator can see. And in the child's eyes, when he stands, he is wearing the Germol jersey — blue and green, the shield on the chest — the kit of the protected.

Reality returns and keeps the gift: the player is back on, moving freely — and in the Germol-branded jersey he plays the final minutes. The ball comes to him. One touch, one strike, the net. The team erupts; the winner is scored. At the fence, both small arms shoot to the sky — not because the team won, but because he watched the armour go on and *he knew.* Product hero: ASL on the football-gold gradient, pack sizes beside the kit bag. *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND
1. Executes the client's scripted Reel 3 beat-for-beat: grass cut → immediate ASL treatment → return in Germol-branded jersey → winning goal.
2. The "no abrasion, no blood" instruction is honoured structurally — the sting is told through the face and the pause, never the wound.
3. The branded jersey enters through the child's imagination first, so the product placement feels earned, then real.
4. First-aid usage of ASL — a core use case none of the other three films carry — completes the usage spread.
5. Football month + SA in the Top 32s: the film rides the season's biggest organic conversation.
6. The sideline kit-bag ritual positions Germol inside a moment the audience already lives — not sponsorship, membership.
7. The armouring visual is the brand Shield asset at its most cinematic.
8. A child witness carrying the belief is the brand's trust story told at maximum stakes.
9. Gold-hour palette with blue/green fantasy keeps R3 colour law inside a new environment.
10. Sign-off lands on triumph — care as the thing that wins games, not just cleans knees.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE
1. Township football at golden hour is beloved, lived iconography — instant place-recognition.
2. Everyone has been the kid at the fence; everyone has been the player who went down.
3. The scroll-stop is the armour spreading from the knee at second 13 — Marvel language on a homely ritual.
4. The jersey reveal inside the fantasy is the gasp beat — kit-drop culture meets storytelling.
5. A clean strike and a real celebration deliver the dopamine of sports content inside an ad.

# FILM 4 — CLOTHES FOR THE SMALLEST
### Family trust · the grown-up kid, mum, and the baby's wash

## STORY SUMMARY

Mid-morning, school holidays, winter light through a net curtain onto a concrete laundry basin. A mother sorts a small mountain of impossibly small clothes — vests, sleepsuits, a blanket with elephants on it — the laundry of the newest member of the household. Beside her stands her older child, twelve or thirteen, sleeves pushed up, here on purpose. Today they asked to help. From the other room, faintly, the baby.

The mother hands her elder the Germol ASL bottle — a small ceremony of trust — and watches them measure the capful into the basin the way they have watched her do it their whole life. The amber falls, blooms, disappears. And the world changes: in the elder child's imagination the basin ignites softly into blue-green light, and as each tiny garment moves through their hands and through the glow, it comes out *kept* — a faint shimmer folded into the fibres, a shield woven in. On the line outside, in their mind's eye, the little clothes ripple with light like prayer flags, protection drying in the winter sun for someone who cannot protect themselves.

The fantasy settles. Reality: wet cuffs, cold hands, the mother and her almost-grown child pegging out the wash together, breath visible. She doesn't say thank you with words; she bumps her shoulder against theirs, and that is the whole conversation. Inside, the baby is lifted and wrapped in the elephant blanket — the one that glowed — and the elder child watches from the doorway with the quiet, enormous pride of someone who has just armoured the smallest knight in the house. Product hero: ASL bottle on the basin edge, both pack sizes. *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND
1. Delivers the client's Reel 4 exactly: a grown-up kid helping mum wash their sibling's baby clothes with ASL — family trust made visible.
2. Laundry-antiseptic usage — a fourth distinct ASL use case, completing the client's awareness spread across all four films.
3. The handover of the bottle dramatizes generational trust in the brand — tomorrow's buyer being formed on camera.
4. Baby clothes are the highest-stakes laundry in any household — trust demonstrated at its maximum, wordlessly.
5. School-holiday brief honoured: the helping child is home, and the help is the story.
6. Skin-safe/nourishment balance implicit — what touches a newborn's skin is the ultimate softness claim, made without a claim.
7. The shoulder-bump close keeps the brand's warmth register — love expressed the way real families express it.
8. The washing-line image extends brand colour into the world poetically without breaking the documentary ground.
9. Pairs with Film 1 as bookends — armour for the body, armour for the smallest body — one protective logic.
10. Sign-off lands over the wrapped baby: "Shields with Love and Care" as literal image. The line and the picture become one.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE
1. A new baby in the house is a universally understood reorganisation of love — stakes need no setup.
2. The teenager who volunteers to help is every parent's quiet dream — aspirational and believable at once.
3. Tiny clothes are an involuntary "aww" — the scroll stops before the fantasy even starts.
4. The glow-through-the-wash at second 12 is the shareable visual — protection you can see folded into fabric.
5. The prayer-flag washing line is the frame people screenshot.
6. Eldest children recognise themselves; mothers recognise their eldest — double identification.
7. The wordless shoulder-bump is emotionally precise — bigger than a hug on camera, because it's truer.
8. The doorway pride beat gives teenagers dignity in a category that usually ignores them.

10. A week later: the elephant blanket, glowing. The softest possible memory of an antiseptic.
