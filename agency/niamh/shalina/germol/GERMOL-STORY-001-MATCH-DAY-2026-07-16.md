## BRAND SUMMARY

Germol is the antiseptic a household already trusts to do the caring jobs — the liquid you tip into water and it protects everyone who dips into that basin, skin and surface alike. It lives by one law, the same one the family films are built on: the product is in the image, used, never posed. No bottle held to camera, no line spoken over it, no lecture about germs. The cloudy swirl of ASL in water is the whole demonstration — the audience knows that swirl, and it says *protected* without a word. Germol's job in the story is to clear the way so the family never has to choose between clean and being on time. The claim lands once, at the very end: *Shields with Love and Care.* Palette holds Germol Blue and Green on white; Rubik Bold; the bottle sits quietly, and that quiet is the point.

## STORY SUMMARY

Match day, late afternoon, and the whole yard smells of smoke and boerewors. The kids are already dressed for the game — green-and-gold, painted grins, too much energy. Someone is still turning meat on the fire, and the work of the day is on everybody's hands: fat, char, the good mess of feeding a crowd. The braai table has taken the brunt of it — plates, marinade, the wear of a long afternoon. Nobody is worried about any of it yet. There is a game coming.

From inside, the commentary starts to climb. You can hear it lift through the door — the pre-kickoff build, the tone that tells a South African household the anthem is minutes away. Heads turn. The pace of the yard changes. And then the call: *"It's starting!"* — and everyone moves at once, converging on the room and the screen, hands still dirty, the table still a mess behind them. This is the pinch every family knows: the moment you want to be clean and seated collides exactly with the moment you cannot afford to miss.

It gets solved the way competence solves things — without a speech. The anchor tips Germol Antiseptic Liquid into a basin of water and gives it a stir. The familiar cloudy swirl forms. In one motion she wipes down the greasy braai table; in the same basin, in the same clouded water, everyone else washes up — one product, double duty, exactly as it is meant to be used. No announcement. No held-up bottle. Just the swirl doing its two jobs while the anthem's opening notes reach the yard.

Table clean, hands clean, everyone seated — and nobody has missed a second of it. The anthem plays to a full couch. And the bottle sits quietly beside the basin, no fuss made about it, having done precisely what it was there to do. On the full, settled room the line lands, once: *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Proves the ASL dual-usage claim (personal hygiene + surface disinfecting) in a single believable action — one basin, one swirl, both jobs — instead of two separate demo reels; it is the product used correctly, on screen, by people who know how.
2. The cloudy swirl is the demonstration and the hero image at once — an ownable, instantly-read product signature that needs no VO and no label-to-camera.
3. Honours the brand law shared with the family work: product in the image, used, never posed; claim held to a single end frame — more credible than a spoken benefit.
4. Lands the women's-month / decision-maker spine without preaching — the wellness lead simply acts; her competence is shown, never narrated.
5. Ties Germol to a high-frequency, high-emotion national occasion (match day) so the brand owns a moment the category usually ignores.
6. Positions Germol as the enabler of togetherness — it removes the clean-vs-on-time trade-off, which is a warmer, stickier role than "kills germs."
7. Carries the winter/gathering season truth (indoor crowds, shared basin, hygiene that keeps the group healthy) exactly as the client brief asks.
8. Gives the channel plan clean cutdowns: the 10s "swirl" moment (tip → stir → wipe → wash), the pack-hero still by the basin, and a WhatsApp-native "it's starting!" beat built to be sent before kickoff.
9. Fits the pack-size ask — the bottle by the basin can show the ASL variant honestly in-scene, with a soap bar plausibly at the sink for the range shot, no posing required.
10. Commercial truth: it moves ASL from an under-the-sink grudge-buy to the thing that keeps the household on time and together — a reason to keep it stocked and reach for it first.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. Every SA household knows this exact yard — smoke, boerewors, kids in green-and-gold — and sees itself inside three seconds.
2. The scroll stops on the smell you can almost smell: the braai, the mess, the crowd. It is pure "that's us."
3. The commentary lifting toward kickoff is a sound that lands in the body — everyone knows what *"it's starting!"* does to a room.
4. The pinch is universal and un-faked: dirty hands, messy table, and no time — they have lived this precise collision.
5. The fix is satisfying because it is quiet — no hero shot, no speech, just someone who has it handled. Competence is a pleasure to watch.
6. The swirl is oddly lovely — people watch things dissolve and bloom in water; it rewards the eye and reads as *clean* instantly.
7. They trust it because nobody performs the product at them — the bottle sits there like it sits in their own kitchen.
8. It flatters the audience: the person who solves it is one of them, and the story assumes they already know how to use the stuff.
9. They send it to the family group with "get there before kickoff" — it is built to be shared in the ten minutes before a game.
10. A week later they remember one image — the cloudy swirl by the basin — and one line, *Shields with Love and Care.*
