## BRAND SUMMARY

Germol is the antiseptic a household already trusts to do the caring jobs — the liquid you tip into water so it protects everyone who dips into that basin, skin and surface alike. It lives by one law, the same one the family films are built on: the product is in the image, used, never posed. No bottle held to camera, no line spoken over it, no lecture about germs. The cloudy swirl of ASL in water is the whole demonstration — the audience knows that swirl, and it says *protected* without a word. Germol's job in the story is to clear the way so the family never has to choose between clean and on time. The claim lands once, at the end: *Shields with Love and Care.* Germol Blue and Green on white; Rubik Bold; the bottle sits quietly, and that quiet is the point.

## STORY SUMMARY

Match day, late afternoon, and the whole yard smells of smoke and boerewors. The kids are already dressed for the game — green-and-gold, painted grins, too much energy — and none of them is stiller than **Sipho**, seven years old, orbiting the adults, watching everything the way small boys do on a day that feels big. Someone is still turning meat on the fire. The work of the afternoon is on everyone's hands: fat, char, the good mess of feeding a crowd. The braai table has taken the brunt of it. Nobody is worried about any of it yet. There is a game coming.

From inside, the commentary starts to climb — you can hear it lift through the door, the pre-kickoff build that tells a South African household the anthem is minutes away. Heads turn. The pace of the yard changes. Then the call: **"It's starting!"** — and everyone moves at once, converging on the screen, hands still dirty, the table still a mess behind them. This is the pinch every family knows: the moment you want to be clean and seated collides exactly with the moment you cannot afford to miss.

**Ma** solves it the way competence solves things — without a speech. She tips Germol Antiseptic Liquid into a basin of water and gives it a stir. The familiar cloudy swirl blooms. In one motion she wipes down the greasy braai table; in the same basin, in the same clouded water, the family washes up — one product, double duty, exactly as it is meant to be used. And it is **Sipho** who watches it happen: his eyes track the swirl, then the table going clean, then the hands going clean, and you see the small arithmetic land behind his face — *same water, both jobs.* No announcement. No held-up bottle. Just a boy understanding something, and the anthem's first notes reaching the yard.

Table clean, hands clean, everyone seated — and nobody has missed a second of it. The anthem plays to a full couch. Sipho drops in between the grown-ups, hands clean, still half-looking back at the basin. And the bottle sits quietly beside it, no fuss made, having done exactly what it was there to do. On the full, settled room the line lands, once: *Shields with Love and Care.*

## WHY THIS WORKS · BRAND

1. Proves the ASL dual-usage claim (personal hygiene + surface disinfecting) in a single believable action — one basin, one swirl, both jobs — instead of two separate demo reels; the product used correctly, on screen, by people who know how.
2. The cloudy swirl is the demonstration and the hero image at once — an ownable, instantly-read product signature that needs no VO and no label-to-camera.
3. The witness (Sipho) lands the claim through a child's eyes, not a spoken benefit — the audience overhears the truth, which is more credible and more shareable than being told it.
4. Honours the shared brand law: product in the image, used, never posed; claim held to a single end frame.
5. Lands the women's-month / decision-maker spine without preaching — Ma simply acts; her competence is shown, never narrated.
6. Ties Germol to a high-frequency, high-emotion national occasion (match day) so the brand owns a moment the category usually ignores.
7. Positions Germol as the enabler of togetherness — it removes the clean-vs-on-time trade-off, a warmer, stickier role than "kills germs."
8. Builds the series engine on the Beat Bible's witness-converts law: this film's watcher is next film's doer — Sipho earns a sequel before a word of it is written.
9. Gives the channel plan clean cutdowns: the 10s "swirl" moment (tip → stir → wipe → wash), the pack-hero still by the basin, and a WhatsApp-native "it's starting!" beat built to be sent before kickoff.
10. Commercial truth: it moves ASL from an under-the-sink grudge-buy to the thing that keeps the household on time and together — a reason to keep it stocked and reach for it first.

## WHY THIS WORKS · AUDIENCE

1. Every SA household knows this exact yard — smoke, boerewors, kids in green-and-gold — and sees itself inside three seconds.
2. The scroll stops on the smell you can almost smell: the braai, the mess, the crowd. Pure "that's us."
3. The commentary lifting toward kickoff is a sound that lands in the body — everyone knows what *"it's starting!"* does to a room.
4. The pinch is universal and un-faked: dirty hands, messy table, no time — they have lived this precise collision.
5. Watching a child work it out is the warmest way in — parents recognise their own kid clocking how the house runs, and feel it in the chest.
6. The swirl is oddly lovely — people watch things dissolve and bloom in water; it rewards the eye and reads as *clean* instantly.
7. They trust it because nobody performs the product at them — the bottle sits there like it sits in their own kitchen.
8. It flatters the audience: the person who solves it is one of them, and the story assumes they already know how to use the stuff.
9. They send it to the family group with "get there before kickoff" — built to be shared in the ten minutes before a game.
10. A week later they remember two things — the cloudy swirl by the basin, and the boy's face working it out — and one line, *Shields with Love and Care.*
