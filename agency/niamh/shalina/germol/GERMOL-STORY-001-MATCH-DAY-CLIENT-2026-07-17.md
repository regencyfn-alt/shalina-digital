### The Story

Match day, late afternoon, and the whole yard smells of smoke and boerewors. The kids are already dressed for the game — green-and-gold, painted grins, too much energy. None of them is stiller than Sipho, seven years old, orbiting the grown-ups, watching everything the way small boys do on a day that feels big. Someone is still turning meat on the fire, and the work of the afternoon is on everybody's hands: fat, char, the good mess of feeding a crowd. The braai table has taken the brunt of it. Nobody is worried about any of that yet. There's a game coming.

From inside, the commentary starts to climb. You can hear it lift through the door — that pre-kickoff build every South African household knows, the sound that means the anthem is minutes away. Heads turn. The pace of the yard changes. Then the call: "It's starting!" — and everyone moves at once, converging on the screen with dirty hands and a messy table left behind them. It's the moment every family knows too well: wanting to be clean and seated, exactly when you can't afford to miss a thing.

Ma solves it the way people solve things when they know their home — without a fuss. She tips Germol Antiseptic Liquid into a basin of water and gives it a stir, and the familiar cloudy swirl blooms. In one motion she wipes down the greasy table; in the same basin, in the same clouded water, the family washes up. One bottle, doing both jobs, exactly the way it's meant to be used. And it's Sipho who catches it — his eyes follow the swirl, then the table going clean, then the hands going clean, and you can almost see him work it out: same water, both jobs. No announcement. No bottle held up to the room. Just a boy understanding something, and the first notes of the anthem reaching the yard.

Table clean, hands clean, everyone seated — and nobody has missed a second. The anthem plays to a full couch. Sipho drops in between the grown-ups, hands clean, still half-looking back at the basin. And the bottle sits quietly beside it, no fuss made, having done exactly what it was there for.
