# FILM 1 — DEPARTURE
### Personal hygiene · the capful in the bath · client Reel 1

## STORY SUMMARY

A cold school-holiday morning, the bathroom the warmest room in the house. A mother runs a shallow bath for her son, six years old, towel around his shoulders like a station master's cape — and he has brought his passenger: the teal Germol Express toy, set carefully on the windowsill, facing the water. It always watches the bath. She shows him the Germol ASL bottle — he insists on the showing — and pours one capful. The amber blooms, vanishes, and the steam rises off the water.

The steam is where the crossing happens. It thickens, climbs the tiles, and becomes cloud — and out of the cloud, at full size and perfectly calm about it, the Germol Express slides into the bathroom, doors open, lamps warm. The boy is already aboard. So is she. The bath has become the departure pool of a cloud country: soft white steam-hills, pastel spa-pools steaming below the track, and the little germ-creatures — pink, moss-green, mustard, fuzzy as bath sponges — lounging in the far pools, too comfortable to be any trouble, waving lazily as the train hums past above them. The boy leans out of the window into the warm air; his mother holds the back of his jumper the way mothers do even in imaginary countries. On the floor of the real bathroom, tiny and faithful, the toy Express still circles the bath mat.

The return is gentle: steam thins, tiles reappear, and he is being lifted out, wrapped, warm, clean — a passenger arriving rather than a boy leaving a bath. On the sill, the toy has stopped exactly at its station. Product hero: ASL bottle at the bath edge, both pack sizes. *Shields with Love and Care.*

# FILM 2 — THE SHINING TRACK
### Surface cleaning & disinfecting · client Reel 2

## STORY SUMMARY

Early, dark-early. The working mother — doek, house coat, spray bottle filled from the Germol ASL cap by muscle memory — starts her round while the house sleeps. On the rug behind her, mid-floor, her child's teal Germol Express sits on its circle of track, still from the night before. She wipes the counter in one long clean stroke.

And the stroke leaves *track*. Gleaming, golden-blue rail, laid exactly where her cloth has been. She can't see it. But the toy train's lamp flicks on. As she moves through the kitchen — door handle, tap, light switch, table — every wiped surface extends the line, a shining railway being laid through the house by the ordinary movement of her arm. The toy pulls off its circle and follows her route. And then the crossing: the Express at full size now runs the house — down the counter-line, curving through the doorway she has just made safe, and in the lit window of the carriage is her child in pyjamas, conductor's whistle in hand, riding the morning's work like a grand opening. Each cleaned surface is a station: LIGHT SWITCH HALT. TAP JUNCTION. The germ-creatures stand well back on the platforms, holding tiny suitcases, entirely resigned, some waving. She finishes her round in reality — one woman, one bottle — while behind her, in the child's world, an entire railway network comes online.

She caps the spray, turns, and finds the child in the doorway, whistle still in mouth. The quiet smile that means *I see you.* A small thought-cloud rises over her head as she looks at the finished kitchen: *"What should I do with my extra time?"* The child holds up the toy train. Answer supplied. Product hero: ASL bottle and spray, pack sizes on the shelf. *Shields with Love and Care.*

# FILM 3 — THE WINNING RETURN
### First aid on the pitch · client Reel 3, beats mandatory

## STORY SUMMARY

Township pitch, golden hour, the game that matters. A teenage player goes down chasing a fifty-fifty — a grass cut on the knee, nothing worse, no blood, just the sting and the pause. At the fence, both hands in the wire: his six-year-old brother, who came with the toy — the teal Germol Express clutched under one arm, brought to every game for luck.

The coach's kit bag opens. Germol ASL, cotton wool, the practiced dab every football family knows. And at the touch of the antiseptic, the little brother's world takes over: a whistle sounds from nowhere, and the Express arrives *at the touchline* — full-size, gleaming, utterly unbothered by the impossibility — through the golden dust. The injured player boards; the carriage lamps warm him; and the train takes him on one slow, magnificent healing lap of the pitch while the game holds its breath. Through the carriage window we see it happen: the sting leaving his face, his shoulders coming back, and the kit changing — when he steps down onto the grass he is wearing the Germol jersey, blue and green, the shield on the chest. On the embankment beyond the far touchline, the germ-creatures have gathered like a beaten team's supporters, waving him back on with little flags. Fair play to them.

Reality: he jogs back into position — in the Germol jersey — and the ball finds him. One touch, one strike, the net. The winner. The team buries him in the celebration, and at the fence the smallest supporter erupts, toy train held above his head like a cup. He watched the Express come for his brother. He knew. Product hero: ASL against the gold, pack sizes by the kit bag. *Shields with Love and Care.*

# FILM 4 — THE NIGHT TRAIN
### Family trust · the baby's wash · client Reel 4

## STORY SUMMARY

Late afternoon into evening, winter light going honey-coloured. At the concrete basin, a mother and her almost-grown child — twelve, sleeves up, here because they asked to be — wash the smallest laundry in the house: vests, sleepsuits, the elephant blanket. The newest member of the family can be heard, faintly, from the other room. The mother hands over the Germol ASL bottle — the small ceremony of being trusted with it — and the elder measures the capful into the water exactly as they have watched her do it all their life.

They peg the wash out as the light goes down: a line of tiny clothes against a rising moon. And the line is where the crossing happens. As the last peg closes, the washing line lifts into the sky and becomes *track* — the moon-track — a single silver-gold rail curving in front of an enormous, patient moon. The Express runs it as a night train, quiet as a lullaby, and in its lit carriages ride the passengers: the little vests and sleepsuits, each in its own warm window like sleeping travellers, the elephant blanket in the last carriage, and the elder child as the guard, walking the aisle, checking on every tiny passenger. Far below, germ-country is asleep — pastel hills dark and soft, one or two creatures in nightcaps waving drowsily up at the train that never stops for them anymore.

Return: the nursery, night. The baby is lifted and wrapped in the elephant blanket — the one that rode the last carriage — and settles instantly. In the doorway, the mother bumps her shoulder against her eldest's: the whole conversation. On the nursery shelf, the toy Express stands parked, facing the cot. On watch. Product hero: ASL on the basin edge, both pack sizes. *Shields with Love and Care.*
