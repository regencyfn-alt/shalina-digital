# FILM 1 — THE HARBOUR
### Personal hygiene · the capful in the bath · client Reel 1 · Vehicle: THE GERMOL STEAMER (toy boat)

## STORY SUMMARY

A cold school-holiday morning, the bathroom the warmest room in the house. A mother runs a shallow bath for her son, six years old, towel around his shoulders — and he has brought his crew: a small teal-and-white toy steamer, Germol shield on the funnel, launched onto the bathwater the moment it is deep enough. It always sails the bath first. She shows him the Germol ASL bottle — he insists on the showing — and pours one capful. The amber blooms through the water, vanishes, and the steam comes up off the surface.

The steam is where the crossing happens. It thickens along the waterline and becomes sea-mist — and the toy's little funnel answers with a real chuff of white. The bath is suddenly a harbour at morning: the taps are the harbour lights, the soap dish is the quay, and through the mist, at full size and perfectly calm about it, the Germol Steamer comes in — brass rails, warm portholes, gangway down. The boy is already aboard in a captain's towel-cape; his mother stands beside him at the wheel, one hand on his shoulder the way mothers do even in imaginary harbours. Below them the water is a pastel bay, and the germ-creatures — pink, moss-green, mustard, fuzzy as bath sponges — bob in the far shallows on little inflatable rings, too comfortable to be any trouble, waving as the ship glides past. On the real bathwater, tiny and faithful, the toy steamer keeps its own course around the boy's knees.

The return is gentle: the mist thins to steam, the quay becomes the soap dish, and he is being lifted out, wrapped, warm, clean — a sailor coming ashore rather than a boy leaving a bath. The toy sits moored against the bath edge, journey done. Product hero: ASL bottle at the bath edge, both pack sizes. *Shields with Love and Care.*

# FILM 2 — THE SHINING TRACK
### Surface cleaning & disinfecting · client Reel 2 · Vehicle: THE GERMOL EXPRESS (toy train — the Express's one film)

## STORY SUMMARY

Early, dark-early. The working mother — doek, house coat, spray bottle filled from the Germol ASL cap by muscle memory — starts her round while the house sleeps. On the rug behind her, mid-floor, her child's teal Germol Express sits on its circle of track, still from the night before. She wipes the counter in one long clean stroke.

And the stroke leaves *track*. Gleaming, golden-blue rail, laid exactly where her cloth has been. She can't see it. But the toy train's lamp flicks on. As she moves through the kitchen — door handle, tap, light switch, table — every wiped surface extends the line, a shining railway being laid through the house by the ordinary movement of her arm. The toy pulls off its circle and follows her route. And then the crossing: the Express at full size now runs the house — down the counter-line, curving through the doorway she has just made safe — and in the lit window of the carriage is her child in pyjamas, conductor's whistle in hand, riding the morning's work like a grand opening. Each cleaned surface is a station: LIGHT SWITCH HALT. TAP JUNCTION. The germ-creatures stand well back on the platforms, holding tiny suitcases, entirely resigned, some waving. She finishes her round in reality — one woman, one bottle — while behind her, in the child's world, an entire railway network comes online. The toy, faithful, keeps circling the rug through all of it.

She caps the spray, turns, and finds the child in the doorway, whistle still in mouth. The quiet smile that means *I see you.* A small thought-cloud rises over her head as she looks at the finished kitchen: *"What should I do with my extra time?"* The child holds up the toy train. Answer supplied. Product hero: ASL bottle and spray, pack sizes on the shelf. *Shields with Love and Care.*

# FILM 3 — THE FLYPAST
### First aid on the pitch · client Reel 3, beats mandatory · Vehicle: THE GERMOL FLYER (toy plane)

## STORY SUMMARY

Township pitch, golden hour, the game that matters. A teenage player goes down chasing a fifty-fifty — a grass cut on the knee, nothing worse, no blood, just the sting and the pause. At the fence, both hands in the wire: his six-year-old brother, who came with his co-pilot — a small teal toy plane, Germol shield on the tail, flown to every game in one outstretched hand.

The coach's kit bag opens. Germol ASL, cotton wool, the practiced dab every football family knows. And at the touch of the antiseptic, the little brother's arm lifts the toy into the air — and the sky answers. Out of the golden haze above the pitch comes the Germol Flyer at full size: a gleaming teal-and-white aircraft, banking low and unhurried over the goalposts, its engine note more like a purr than a roar. The injured player looks up from the touchline — and he is aboard, window seat over the wing, watching his own pitch wheel below him. One slow, magnificent healing circuit in the gold: the sting leaves his face, the shoulders come back, and when the Flyer sweeps the far touchline it lays a banner of clean white contrail — and through it, stepping back down onto the grass, he is in the Germol jersey, blue and green, shield on the chest. On the embankment the germ-creatures have gathered like a beaten team's supporters, little flags out, waving him back on. Fair play to them. At the fence, the toy plane never stopped flying figure-eights in the small hand.

Reality: he jogs back into position — in the jersey — and the ball finds him. One touch, one strike, the net. The winner. The team buries him in the celebration, and the smallest supporter at the fence flies the toy plane straight up over his head, a victory climb. He watched the Flyer come for his brother. He knew. Product hero: ASL against the gold, pack sizes by the kit bag. *Shields with Love and Care.*

# FILM 4 — THE MOON BALLOON
### Family trust · the baby's wash · client Reel 4 · Vehicle: THE GERMOL BALLOON (cot-mobile hot-air balloon)

## STORY SUMMARY

Late afternoon into evening, winter light going honey-coloured. At the concrete basin, a mother and her almost-grown child — twelve, sleeves up, here because they asked to be — wash the smallest laundry in the house: vests, sleepsuits, the elephant blanket. The newest member of the family can be heard, faintly, from the other room — where, above the cot, a little mobile turns: a tiny teal hot-air balloon, Germol shield on its envelope, drifting its slow circle. The mother hands over the Germol ASL bottle — the small ceremony of being trusted with it — and the elder measures the capful into the water exactly as they have watched her do it all their life.

They peg the wash out as the light goes down: a line of tiny clothes against a rising moon. And the line is where the crossing happens. As the last peg closes, a warm glow swells behind the sheets — and up over the washing line, huge and patient and quiet as a lullaby, rises the Germol Balloon, its envelope lit from within like a lamp. Its basket is wide and blanket-soft, and in it ride the passengers: the little vests and sleepsuits tucked in rows like sleeping travellers, the elephant blanket folded on top, and the elder child as the pilot, one hand on the burner cord, checking on every tiny passenger. The balloon drifts up the sky in front of an enormous moon, the washing line its launch field. Far below, germ-country is asleep — pastel hills dark and soft, one or two creatures in nightcaps waving drowsily up at the light passing over, a country the balloon never needs to land in anymore. In the nursery, the real mobile keeps turning its small circle above the cot.

Return: the nursery, night. The baby is lifted and wrapped in the elephant blanket — the one that rode the basket — and settles instantly. In the doorway, the mother bumps her shoulder against her eldest's: the whole conversation. Above the cot, the little balloon turns, lit by the nightlight. On watch. Product hero: ASL on the basin edge, both pack sizes. *Shields with Love and Care.*
