## 1. What This Campaign Is

Malaria doesn't announce itself. It arrives and takes. A day from a mother's life. A week from a man's hustle. A graduation, a first step, a moment on stage — gone while someone is flat in bed, burning up, waiting to get better.

Bounce Back is not a product campaign. It is a testimony campaign.

Every execution asks the same question in reverse: *what would you have missed if you hadn't bounced back in time?* The answer is always personal. Always specific. Always earned. And always delivered in the first person, by the person who lived it.

The product is the hinge — Shal'Artem is what makes the bounce-back possible. But the story is the moment they almost missed. That moment is gold.

---

## 2. The Formula

Every Bounce Back execution runs on a single structure:

> **"I bounced back [connector] [the moment]."**

Three parts. Non-negotiable.

| Part | Function | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **"I bounced back"** | The anchor. Always these exact words. Never paraphrased. | "I bounced back" |
| **[connector]** | The bridge to the moment. "to", "in time to", "and" | "in time to see" / "to walk across" / "and got on stage" |
| **[the moment]** | The specific, irreplaceable thing they would have missed | "my son take his first steps" / "collect my degree" / "rock the stadium" |

**The connector and moment are in gold (#C68A1E). The rest is black.**  
The gold is the thing at stake. Gold = what almost didn't happen.

## 3. The Visual Language

### Photography
- **Real people, real moments.** Documentary photography only. No posed stock. No studio shots.
- **The setting is always a real occasion** — the actual graduation ceremony, the actual living room floor, the actual stage with the actual crowd behind.
- **The subject faces the camera or is in full action** — never passive, never lying down. They are already bounced back when we see them.
- **Warm, saturated, human.** Natural light preferred. Kodak Vision3 look where possible — warm shadows, held highlights, organic grain.

### Typography
- **Headline:** Playfair Display 800, MIXED CASE (not all caps). The text is conversational testimony, not a poster slogan.
- **Gold phrases:** Same weight, same face, different colour — #C68A1E
- **Quotation mark ("):** Present at opening. Closing " at end of quote only when the quote closes in frame.
- **Eyebrow:** Not present in this format — the image IS the eyebrow.
- **No Shal'Artem blue in the headline block.** The blue is structural (product packaging, end cards). The testimony lives in black and gold only.

### Layout
- **Portrait format.** 4:5 (Instagram/FB) or 9:16 (Stories/Reels).
- **Text block: left-aligned, upper-left quadrant.**
- **Subject: right-side dominant or centre, not obscured by text.**
- **No gradient overlays.** No solid colour panels. Text sits directly on the photograph — the image must be legible enough to hold it.
- **Shal'Artem DT/12T/18T pack:** present at bottom-right or end card. Never superimposed on the testimony text.

## 4. The 8-Video Sprint

Eight 15-second social films. One testimony per film. Eight different people, eight different moments, one shared beginning: *I bounced back.*

### The Film Structure (15 seconds)
Every Bounce Back film runs the same engine:

| Beat | Duration | What Happens |
|------|----------|--------------|
| **01 · The Weight** | 0–4s | We see the person before they're okay. The closed curtain, the still bedroom, the quiet that doesn't fit them. No dialogue. We feel what was taken. |
| **02 · The Turn** | 4–7s | Shal'Artem. Tablet visible. Hand reaching. Or taken. Product truth, no words needed. |
| **03 · The Bounce** | 7–12s | They're back. In it. The moment they would have missed, happening in real time. |
| **04 · The Line** | 12–15s | Text over still or slow motion: *"I bounced back [in time to/to/and] [the moment]."* Shal'Artem pack. "Never Miss Your Moment." |

### The Eight Stories
These are the confirmed scenarios based on existing campaign imagery and Nigerian/Ghanaian life contexts:

## 5. Production Rules

### What the testimony must do
- First person, always. "I bounced back." Not "She bounced back." Not "They bounce back."
- The moment must be specific. "I bounced back to be there" is not a Bounce Back line. "I bounced back in time to hold my daughter at her naming ceremony" is.
- The moment in gold must be the one irreplaceable thing — the thing that only happens once and cannot be rescheduled.

### What the testimony must never do
- Never mention malaria by name. The product carries the clinical truth. The campaign carries the human truth.
- Never show sickness, suffering, or a hospital bed. We enter after the turn.
- Never use "fast relief" or "quick recovery" language. That's a product claim. We don't make claims. We tell stories.
- Never use a generic "moment" — a vague life win, a smile, a thumbs-up. It has to be something the audience can identify as irreplaceable.

### Product visibility
- Shal'Artem DT / 12T / 18T must appear in every execution — either at Beat 02 (the turn) or the end card.
- Label must be legible. Not background texture — in-hand, hero placement.
- NMA-endorsed (Nigeria) / GMA-endorsed (Ghana) messaging applies per market. Do not mix market compliance claims.

### Format
- **9:16 vertical primary** (Reels, TikTok, Stories)
- **4:5 square secondary** (Feed, Facebook)
- **Film:** Seedance 2.0 · Drama · Audio on · diegetic only · 22.5 credits/clip
- **Stills:** Nano Banana Pro / Soul Cinema · Reference image locked per character

---

## 7. What This Campaign Is Not

- It is **not** a before/after — we never show the sick person. We show the person who got back.
- It is **not** aspirational-glossy — these are real moments from real lives, not lifestyle advertising.
- It is **not** a product demo — Shal'Artem does not appear in the moment. It appears at the turn. It enables; it does not star.
- It is **not** general — "feeling better" is not a Bounce Back line. Specific irreplaceable moments only.
- It is **not** sad — we are in the joy of the return. The weight of what was almost missed is felt, not shown. The film lives in gratitude and relief, not grief.
